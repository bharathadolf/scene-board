# What else could go in

Eight events, 115 scenes, about three hours twenty. This file is the list of
what is *not* in it yet: events from the sources that would fit this particular
film, sequences that can be built out of scenes that already exist, and the
attractive material that should stay out, with the reason.

Nothing here is on the board. Codes `E09` and up are proposals; the scenes they
would need are not written.

---

## The test a candidate has to pass

A story event can be magnificent and still be wrong for this film. Four
questions, in order:

1. **Can he be unseen in it?** Every scene before `E06-18` must be filmable as
   *Not present*, *Spoken of*, *Shadow or sound*, *Hands*, *Effect*, or a
   *Reflection in the witness's face*. An event whose whole point is watching
   Krishna do something is out.
2. **Does it reach Rukmini?** She is the protagonist. E01–E05 exist because
   something in them travels to Vidarbha — a mural, a bard, a sailor, a
   soldier's tally. A candidate needs a carrier that can reach her, or it is a
   different film's scene.
3. **Does it come before the abduction?** Nothing follows E07. Material whose
   place in the source is after the wedding can only come in as a plant, a
   rumour, or a transplant into an earlier scene.
4. **Does somebody ordinary feel the result?** The film's subject is
   *loka-kalyāṇam* — world-welfare. The best candidates end on a person who
   benefits without knowing the cause.

The candidates below are sorted by how well they answer those four.

## 1. Strong candidates

### C1 · Kaṁsa and the eighth child — proposed **E09**
**కంస వధ** · suggested 8 scenes · ≈ 14 min · SB 10.1–10.4, 10.42–10.44

*The story.* A wedding procession, and a voice out of the air telling Kaṁsa
that his cousin's eighth child will kill him. He turns on his own family:
Devakī is imprisoned, and each child she bears is taken and killed. The seventh
is moved; the eighth is born at midnight and exchanged, under a sleep that falls
on an entire city, for a cowherd's daughter — and when Kaṁsa lifts that child
to kill her she slips out of his hands and speaks from the air. Years later a
bow is broken in his city, two wrestlers are put down in his arena, and he dies
on his own floor in front of his court. Two of his wives go home to their
father in Magadha.

*Why it fits.* The voice from the sky is already the film's grammar — it is how
E01 and E02 work — and here it is used against a man instead of for one.
Krishna is an infant for most of it, which is the easiest kind of unseen: a
bundle, a hand, a sound in the dark. The arena can be shot the way the Mathurā
battle is shot, from the crowd.

*The job it does.* It gives E03 its cause. Right now the film opens its war
with two widows explaining a death the audience has not seen [E03-01]; this
makes that scene a pay-off instead of exposition. It also supplies the one
thing the film currently lacks — a reason the Yadus are afraid of kings in the
first place.

*Where it sits.* Before E03 in story order, anywhere in the cut. In the
commercial cut it would replace part of the prologue block.

*What it costs.* Fourteen minutes, and a second mythic prologue competing with
E01 and E02 for the same early real estate. The film already has two origin
events; three is a lot of ground before the woman arrives.

*How it reaches her.* Through the enemy. The widows' version of it is what
Magadha believes, and Rukmī admires Magadha [E03-14] — so she first hears of
Kaṁsa's death as a crime committed by the man she is interested in.

**Verdict: add it, and cut E02 to five scenes to pay for it.**

### C2 · Uddhava goes back to Vṛndāvana — proposed **E10**
**ఉద్ధవ సందేశం** · suggested 6 scenes · ≈ 11 min · SB 10.46–10.47

*The story.* Krishna sends Uddhava to the village he grew up in, carrying a
message to the people he left. Uddhava finds a place that has not moved on: a
foster mother who still cooks for someone who is not coming, cowherds who talk
about him in the present tense, women who argue with the messenger because they
cannot argue with the man. He delivers the message, stays, and comes back with
nothing to report except how much he has been missed.

*Why it fits.* It is the letter, running the other way, and it is an entire
event in which Krishna is nowhere near the camera — the strongest possible
compliance with the film's rule. It is also the only candidate that gives the
film something it does not have at all: the cost of him to people who are not
in the plot.

*The job it does.* It complicates the thing the film is about. Rukmini spends
three hours assembling a man out of rumour; this is the rumour that does not
flatter him. A woman choosing someone who has already left somewhere else is a
harder and better story than a woman choosing a hero.

*Where it sits.* Late in the first half, after E04 and before E06-04 — close
enough to her letter that it is the last thing she learns before she writes.

*What it costs.* Eleven minutes, and a tonal risk: the village is a famous
story and the audience may want more of it than the film can give.

*How it reaches her.* Uddhava's road passes through Vidarbha, or a trader who
was in the village repeats it at a well. She hears that there is a place where
they are still waiting, and she writes anyway. That is the scene.

**Verdict: add it. This is the one that makes the film better rather than
bigger.**

### C3 · Pauṇḍraka, the false Vāsudeva — proposed **E11**
**పౌండ్రక వాసుదేవుడు** · suggested 5 scenes · ≈ 9 min · SB 10.66

*The story.* A king decides that he is the real Vāsudeva. He has the marks
made: the disc, the conch, the club, the lotus, the garland, the mark on the
chest, the dark cloth. His court agrees with him, because courts do. He sends a
messenger to Dvārakā demanding that the impostor surrender the name. The
challenge is accepted. His own weapons are turned on him, and his head is
delivered back into his own city, where the people who had been calling him
Vāsudeva look at it.

*Why it fits.* This is a film about a man whose face is never shown, in which a
sage hands out a checklist of marks by which to recognise him [E05-01, E05-16].
Pauṇḍraka is that checklist worn by the wrong person. The whole event can be
shot on him — the fake is fully visible, which is exactly why the real one does
not need to be.

*The job it does.* It tells the audience that the marks are not the man, right
before it asks them to identify him by his marks at the temple door. And
Pauṇḍraka is already standing in the film: he is on the list of kings who swear
to fight Krishna if he comes for the bride [E06-09].

*Where it sits.* Second half of the first half, before the coalition gathers.
In the source it falls after the marriage; this film is non-linear and the
event has no dependency on the wedding, so moving it costs nothing but a note
in the chronology.

*What it costs.* Nine minutes, and one chronological liberty.

*How it reaches her.* A herald reads Pauṇḍraka's claim aloud in her father's
hall, and the hall takes it seriously. She does not.

**Verdict: add it. It is cheap, it is funny, and it arms the audience for
[E06-18].**

### C4 · The two widows — proposed **E12** *(written for this film)*
**అస్తి, ప్రాప్తి** · suggested 4 scenes · ≈ 7 min

*The story.* Asti and Prāpti, before they are the scene that starts a war. Two
sisters married into Mathurā for politics, living with a man the city is afraid
of; then the morning they are made widows; then the long road home to a father
who loves them and will burn a country to prove it. Last, seventeen years on,
one of them watching the army leave again and not watching it come back.

*Why it fits.* It is four scenes with no Krishna in them at all, and it turns
the film's antagonist from a man who cannot learn into a man who cannot stop
grieving in public.

*The job it does.* It pairs with Rukmini. Both strands are women handed to men
by their families for borders; one of them writes a letter, the other inherits
a war. The film's argument is sharper with the other outcome visible.

*Where it sits.* Wrapped around E03 — the first scene before [E03-01], the
last one against the seventeenth chalk mark [E03-09].

*What it costs.* Almost nothing: seven minutes, four scenes, no CG above tier
1, no new locations beyond a Magadha interior.

*How it reaches her.* It does not need to. It is the only strand in the film
allowed to exist for the enemy's sake — and it can still be delivered, as the
story a visiting woman tells in Kuṇḍina about what happens to daughters who are
married for a border.

**Verdict: add it. Cheapest strong candidate on the list.**

### C5 · Akrūra in the river — proposed **E13**
**అక్రూరుని దర్శనం** · suggested 4 scenes · ≈ 7 min · SB 10.38–10.39

*The story.* A man is sent to fetch somebody and spends the whole journey
arguing with himself about whether he will be allowed to see him. He stops at a
river to perform his rites, goes under the water, and sees — in the water,
where he is alone — what he has been doubting. He comes up, looks around at an
ordinary riverbank, goes under again to check, and then rides on and tells
nobody, because there is no way to say it.

*Why it fits.* The film already has one scene of a witness whose face is the
whole shot [E05-07]. This is that method at its purest: the vision is *in a
reflection*, which the board's own grammar lists as a legal way to show him,
and the man it happens to cannot prove it afterwards.

*The job it does.* It is the film's answer to the question the audience is
asking by the second hour — does anyone actually see this man? — and the answer
is: one person did, underwater, and said nothing.

*Where it sits.* Anywhere in the middle hour. It is a four-scene breath between
two sieges.

*What it costs.* Seven minutes and one water-vision shot that has to be good,
because the whole event is that shot.

*How it reaches her.* It cannot be told convincingly, and that is the point —
it reaches her as a story nobody believes, from a man she is told is sensible.

**Verdict: add it if the film has seven minutes. Strong, small, optional.**

### C6 · The seventeen, from Magadha — proposed **E14** *(written for this film)*
**మగధ వైపు** · suggested 5 scenes · ≈ 9 min

*The story.* The same war as E03, shot entirely from the losing side: a map
room where the plan is always sound, a quartermaster who has done this sixteen
times, a son-in-law being consoled, a king refusing penance because his friends
talk him out of it [E03-08], and a muster where the men are younger each time.

*Why it fits.* It needs no Krishna at all, and it makes the chalk marks on
Mathurā's wall [E03-09] hurt from both sides.

*The job it does.* It is the only way to make seventeen repetitions dramatic
rather than merely clever. The film currently tells the audience that the enemy
cannot learn; this shows them why — every defeat is explained away by somebody
who loves him.

*Where it sits.* Intercut with E03. On the board it is a second column beside
the sieges.

*What it costs.* Nine minutes, and a risk of sympathy drifting to Magadha.

**Verdict: add it in the master cut, drop it in the commercial cut.**

## 2. Conditional candidates

These do not work as events, but their material is worth taking.

**The Syamantaka jewel** (SB 10.56–57). A jewel that passes from hand to hand
and ruins everyone who grabs it — the film's "seizing is not holding" thesis as
a nine-scene thriller, rhyming directly with the Kaustubha jewel that an unseen
hand takes in [E01-09]. *The problem:* it comes with Jāmbavatī, a second
marriage, into a film built around one woman's choice. *Take instead:* one
scene. A trader in Kuṇḍina tells the story of a jewel and the four men it has
killed, and it lands on Rukmī, who is arranging a sister for a border.

**Rukmiṇī and Krishna's argument** (SB 10.60). In the text, immediately after
the marriage, he teases her: she should have chosen a king, he is penniless, she
was wrong. She faints; he takes it back; it is the most alive conversation
either of them has in the source. *The problem:* it is after the wedding, and
nothing follows E07. *Take instead:* this is the missing line in
[E07-07] — "what he says, and does not say", currently a placeholder. Trim
10.60 to a single exchange on the terrace and the film's last words are hers.

**Sudāmā, the poor brāhmaṇa** (SB 10.80–81). A man who arrives with a handful
of flattened rice, is received like a king, asks for nothing, goes home and
finds his house rebuilt — the purest *loka-kalyāṇam* scene in the whole
Bhāgavata. *The problem:* also after the marriage. *Note:* the film already
has it. The Brahmin who carries the letter [E06-05, E06-06] is received,
honoured, fed, and has his feet pressed by the man he came to petition. If any
of 10.80 is to be used, it belongs inside those two scenes.

**Jarasandha's end** (SB 10.72). Seventeen releases finally explained: he dies
in a wrestling match, split along the seam he was born with, at Krishna's
suggestion. *The problem:* years after this film. *Take instead:* nothing on
screen — but it is why [E03-03] is allowed to be mysterious. One line in the
scene can promise it without showing it.

**Śālva's flying city** (SB 10.76–77) and **Śiśupāla at the Rājasūya**
(SB 10.74). Both are the coalition's revenge, both are sequels, and both are
already planted: Śālva swears the vow in [E06-09], Śiśupāla's hundred offences
are counted from [E08-03]. *Take instead:* leave them planted. They are the
reason the ending can be warm without being final.

## 3. Sequences that need no new writing

These are new arrangements of scenes that already exist. They cost nothing but
an afternoon on the board.

**The three times the voice speaks.** [E01-03] → [E02-04] → [E05-08], and then
[E06-22], where the same voice says something small to one frightened woman on
a chariot. Played as a motif, the fourth one lands like a reveal.

**Four witnesses.** [E01-17] the fisher child, [E04-07] the gate guard,
[E05-07] Mucukunda, [E05-14] the herdsman at the well. Cut together, they are a
five-minute sequence of people describing a man none of them can describe.

**Seventeen.** [E03-02] the first chalk mark, [E03-09] the seventeen, [E03-15]
the soldier who does not know why, [E07-03] the clean wall. The film's longest
arc, and it is four scenes.

**The letter in three hands.** [E06-04] she writes it, [E06-05] the Brahmin
carries it, [E06-06] it is read, [E07-06] a bard sings it back to the kings.
The film's title sequence is sitting in there.

**What she imagined against what she found.** [E04-10] → [E07-02], cut
together at the end as a two-shot rhyme. The only flashback the film needs.

**Three consolations.** [E03-08] the kings talk Jarasandha out of penance,
[E06-23] Jarasandha talks Śiśupāla out of despair, [E06-29] Balarama talks
Rukmini down on the plain. Everybody in this film is consoled; one of them is
changed by it.

**The poison and the plea.** [E01-08] a hand closing on a throat to hold poison
in, [E06-26] a woman at his feet asking him to hold a sword. Same gesture, same
job, three hours apart.

**Two garlands.** [E01-10] Lakṣmī passing every claimant, [E06-14] a woman in
the street lifting a garland toward Rukmini's window. The second one is the
first one, in a crowd.

## 5. If only some of it can be built

Three shortlists, depending on what the cut is for.

**The master cut (currently ≈ 3 h 20).** Add C1 Kaṁsa (14), C2 Uddhava (11),
C3 Pauṇḍraka (9), C4 the widows (7), C6 Magadha (9) = **+50 min**, and pay for
it by cutting E02 to five scenes (−6), E01 to fourteen (−9), and the three
weakest carrier scenes (−4). Net about +31, which puts the film near 3 h 50 —
too long. In practice: **C1, C2 and C4, and the E01–E02 trims**, which lands at
roughly 3 h 30 with a stronger first hour and a real cost attached to the man.

**The commercial cut (150 min).** Only C3 Pauṇḍraka and C4 the widows survive a
runtime that tight — both are short, both are about somebody other than the
hero, and Pauṇḍraka's severed head arriving in his own city is the kind of
beat that cut is built for. Drop C6.

**Nothing at all.** The eight sequences in section 3 need no new material, no
money and no shooting days, and two of them — *the letter in three hands* and
*what she imagined against what she found* — would do more for the film than
any of the six proposed events.
