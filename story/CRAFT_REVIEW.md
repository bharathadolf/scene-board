# Four frameworks against this story, and what each one would change

The four charts measure different things: **character arcs** measure whether
people change, **the thirty-seven situations** measure what kind of trouble the
story is made of, **the Syd Field paradigm** measures where the turns fall on
the clock, and **juxtaposition** measures what the cut puts next to what.

Three of the four say this film is in better shape than it probably feels. One
of them finds a real fault. Everything below is measured against
`DRAFT_SCREENPLAY.md` — 20 sequences, 175 minutes — and `data/orders/braided.json`.

---

## 1 · Character arcs

### What the chart asks

A **primary** arc is positive (the character's false belief is replaced by a
truth), negative (the false belief wins), or flat (the character already holds
the truth and the *world* changes instead). **Secondary** arcs — hero's, coming
of age, redemption — sit on top and come in all three flavours.

### What this film currently has

| | arc as written | the problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rukmini** | **flat, by accident** | She is right in her first scene and right in her last. Nothing she believes is tested. A flat arc is legitimate — but only when the world resists the belief and is visibly changed by it, and that payoff is currently one scene long (`E07-06`, the bard including the letter). |
| **Krishna** | **flat, by design** | Correct. He cannot have an interior arc when the film refuses to show him, and the flat arc is the right shape: he holds a truth nobody understands for seventeen years and the world comes round. Leave it alone. |
| **Rukmī** | negative, incomplete | He loses, but a negative arc is not losing — it is *getting what you wanted and being ruined by it*. He never visibly gets the standing he sold her for. |
| **Balarama** | the film's one true secondary arc | Loyalty tested: he marches an army for a decision he thinks is wrong, then says so to his brother's face. This is the best-built arc in the film and nobody has named it. |
| **Jarasandha** | negative-flat | He cannot learn because his friends talk him out of learning (`E03-08`). That is a complete and rather good arc. |
| **Śiśupāla** | negative, passive | He is never fought, never beaten, never addressed, and that is the point. |

### What would change

**a. Name Rukmini's arc, and make it positive.** The material for a real arc is
already on the board and is not being used as one:

> **The lie she starts with:** *choosing will cost nothing but courage.*
> **The truth she ends with:** *every choice is paid for, and not always by you.*

Three scenes already carry it and need only to be pointed in the same direction:
`E06-22`, where she watches men die under arrows for a letter she wrote —
currently a fright beat, should be the first crack; `E06-26`, where she kneels
for her brother's life; and `E07-05`, the place laid for a brother who will not
come. Plant the lie once in act one — in `D-02` or `NEW-03`, a line where she is
sure that being right is enough — and the arc closes itself.

This costs nothing structurally. It is one planted line and a change of emphasis
in two scenes.

**b. If you keep her flat instead, pay the flat arc properly.** A flat-arc
protagonist is measured by how much the world moves. Then `E07-06` is not a
grace note, it is the climax of her arc and should be staged as one: the kings
who swore the oath hearing her version sung back at them.

**c. Give Rukmī the thing he sold her for, before taking it.** One scene,
movement VI: Jarasandha treating him as an equal in front of the court — the
standing he traded a sister for, in his hands for ninety seconds. Then the shave
means something. Without it he is a man who loses; with it he is a man who wins
and is ruined.

**d. Nobody in this film has a redemption arc, and that is a decision.** Rukmī
is the obvious candidate and the film deliberately refuses him: he builds
Bhojakaṭa and stays. Own it in the writing rather than leaving it as a gap —
`E06-29`, Balarama's consolation, is the right place for someone to say that
some things do not get mended.

## 2 · The thirty-seven dramatic situations

### What the film already holds

The chart is a checklist of trouble. This story is unusually rich against it —
sixteen of the twenty-four listed situations are already on the board:

| # | situation | where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rescue | the abduction read from her side |
| 3 | A miracle of God | Dvārakā rising `E04-04`, the ash `E05-05` |
| 4 | **Entreaty** | the Earth's petition `E02-02`, Mucukunda's prayer `E05-09`, **her letter** `E06-04` |
| 5 | Love's obstacles | the whole second half |
| 6 | Rivalry between unequals | Śiśupāla against a man who will not look at him |
| 7 | **Rivalry between kinsmen** | Rukmī against his sister's choice; Balarama against his brother |
| 8 | **A mystery** | *why does he let the enemy go, seventeen times* |
| 10 | Sacrifice for an ideal | the seventeen releases `E03-03` |
| 11 | Sacrifice for kindred | Śiva at the shore `E01-08`, her plea `E06-26` |
| 12 | Possessed of an ambition | Rukmī |
| 13 | Fatal indiscretion | Indra's garland `E01-01`, Rukmī's oath `E06-24` |
| 14 | Pursuit | `E05-03`, `E06-22` |
| 15 | Rebellion | a daughter writing to the enemy |
| 16 | Enmity between kinsmen | brother and sister, from `E06-03` to Bhojakaṭa |
| 17 / 18 | Effort to obtain · Daring effort | the kings; the Brahmin's ride `E06-05` |
| 19 / 20 | Vengeance · Kindred avenged against kindred | Jarasandha; the two widows `E03-01` |
| 23 | Struggle against God | Kālayavana; Pauṇḍraka, if built |
| **24** | **Abduction** | the spine |

**The film's signature is the kinsmen cluster plus sacrifice** — 7, 11, 16, 20.
Four of its strongest situations are family turning on family, and its three
best scenes are all somebody paying for somebody else. That is worth knowing
because it tells you what to commission more of, and what the poster is about.

### What is absent, and what to do about each

| # | missing | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **9** | **Loving an enemy** | **Build it.** The one real gap. She loves the man her brother has sworn to kill, and the film never makes her *feel* that as a divided loyalty — only as an obstacle. One scene in movement III where she defends him to Rukmī and realises she has just taken a side against her house. |
| 2 | Lost loved ones recovered | **Keep it absent.** The empty chair is the ending's price. Recovering the brother would refund it. |
| 21 | Mistaken jealousy | Keep out. Cheap here, and it would make her smaller. |
| 22 | Involuntary criminal love | Keep out. |

## 3 · The Syd Field paradigm

### The measurement

Field's marks on a 175-minute film, against where the draft actually puts them:

| Field | should fall | the draft | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inciting incident | ≈ 17 | **1** (he walks out) / **45** (she is sold) | **the fault** |
| Plot Point I | ≈ 44 | 45 — she is sold | ✓ |
| Pinch I | ≈ 65 | 63.5 — what he costs the people he left | ✓ |
| Midpoint | ≈ 88 | 88 — the letter sealed, the hour named | ✓ |
| Pinch II | ≈ 108 | 111.5 — the painted wall | ✓ |
| Plot Point II | ≈ 131 | 130 — the taking | ✓ |
| Climax | ≈ 154 | 153 — she kneels, the sword comes down | ✓ |

Six of seven marks land inside three minutes of the paradigm, which was not
planned and is worth not breaking.

### The one fault

**The film has two structures laid over each other.** Krishna's is Field-perfect
from minute one — a man walks out of a gate, a hunter arrives, the hunt turns.
Rukmini's does not start until she appears at 25, and her inciting incident —
being sold — lands at **45, or 26% in**. By the paradigm her life should be
knocked off balance around minute 17.

For an audience that does not know the story, the first 25 minutes therefore
belong to a man they are told, later, is not the protagonist.

### The fix, which costs nothing

Move her entry forward and split her inciting incident in two:

1. **Minute 8** — `NEW-03`, the market, intercut into the cave sequence: she
   corrects a soldier's tally. The audience meets her before they meet the
   mystery.
2. **Minute 17** — the first half of `E06-03`: she overhears the Chedi match
   being *discussed*, not agreed. Her balance is knocked at the paradigm's mark.
3. **Minute 45** — `D-03`, the measuring, stays exactly where it is: the sale is
   agreed and Plot Point I lands untouched.

Sequence 3 (*Seventeen*) loses two minutes and sequence 4 is halved; everything
from 45 onward is unchanged. **One re-order, no new scenes beyond what the draft
already lists.**

### A second reading worth having

Field's act two first half is "the hero reacts". Hers does: 45–88 she gathers
evidence and is acted upon. The second half is "the hero acts" — and she does,
once, at 88, and then is physically carried for forty minutes while other people
fight. That is true to the source and it is the film's biggest structural risk.
The counter is already drafted: give her the decisions inside the passivity —
she chooses to walk out of the temple, she chooses to kneel, she chooses mercy.
Make sure each of those is shot as a decision and not as a reaction.

## 4 · Juxtaposition

The braided order is *already* a juxtaposition engine — that is what the whole
re-order was. Measured against the eight types:

| type | used? | where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Time** | heavily | the braid itself: ages apart, cut together |
| **Conceptual** | heavily | Lakṣmī choosing (84) into the lift (85) |
| **Idea / theme** | heavily | choosing against seizing, all film |
| **Visual** | yes | the garland, the chalk marks, the lamp, the empty hands |
| **Action** | yes | the duel against her stillness |
| **Setting** | yes | inland Kuṇḍina against the city in the sea |
| **Tonal** | **thin** | one real instance: Jarasandha consoling the ruined bridegroom while the chariot gets away |
| **Character** | **thin** | no deliberate foil for Rukmini |

### What would change

**a. Build the character juxtaposition.** `CANDIDATES.md` already proposes it
without calling it that: **C4, the two widows.** Asti and Prāpti are Rukmini's
exact foils — women handed to men for a border. One writes a letter; the others
inherit a war. Four scenes, no Krishna in them, and the film's argument doubles
in strength because the alternative outcome is on screen.

**b. Sharpen the tonal juxtaposition you have.** Three consolations already rhyme
across the film (`E03-08`, `E06-23`, `E06-29`) — everybody in this story is
talked down, and only one of them is changed by it. Cut them to echo each other
deliberately: same staging, same distance, three different outcomes.

| | change | cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Move Rukmini's entry to minute 8 and her inciting incident to 17**, keeping the sale at 45 | a re-order; no new material |

| 3 | **Give Rukmī ninety seconds of the standing he sold her for**, before the shave | one new scene |

| 5 | **Build C4, the two widows**, as the deliberate foil | four scenes, ≈ 7 min |
| 6 | **Stage the three consolations as a rhyme** | staging, not writing |
| 7 | **Shoot her three second-half choices as decisions**, not reactions | direction note |

Items 1, 2, 6 and 7 cost no runtime at all.

## 6 · What these frameworks would push you toward, and should not

- **Giving Krishna an arc.** Every arc chart wants the lead to change. This one
  cannot and must not — the withheld face *is* the device.
- **Redeeming Rukmī.** The situations chart offers "lost loved ones recovered"
  and the arc chart offers redemption. Both would refund the ending's cost.
- **Resolving the empty chair.** Same mistake, smaller.
- **Breaking the Field marks to fix the opening.** Six of seven turns are already
  on the money. Fix the protagonist's entry by moving *her*, not by moving them.
- **Adding situations for completeness.** Sixteen of twenty-four is not a score
  to improve; a film with all thirty-seven would be a mess.
