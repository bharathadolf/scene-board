## How to read the entries

Each elaborated entry carries one field line:

`minutes · action or drama · CG tier 1–5 · how Krishna appears · rasa · POV · place`

**How Krishna appears** is the law of this film: before `E06-18` he may only be
*Not present*, *Spoken of*, *Shadow or sound*, *Hands*, *Effect* or a
*Reflection in the witness's face*. **CG tier** runs 1 (nothing) to 5 (a full
visual-effects scene). **Rasa** is the register the scene is played in: *Vīra*
heroic, *Bhayānaka* fear, *Raudra* fury, *Adbhuta* wonder, *Karuṇa* grief,
*Śānta* stillness, *Śṛṅgāra* love, *Hāsya* comedy. A **carrier** scene is one
of the ordinary people — the water-carrier, the potter, the ferryman — who feel
the result and never learn the cause.

# 2 · The scenes, elaborated

## E01 — Kṣīra Sāgara Manthanam — the churning of the Ocean of Milk

*19 scenes · ≈ 33.5 min · scenes 1–19 in the list above*

### 1 · `E01-01` · The garland and the curse
`1–2 min` · **drama** · CG tier 3 · Krishna: *Not present* · rasa *Raudra*  
**POV** Indra · **Place** Indra’s court, heaven · **Genre** Mythological epic, Fantasy, Fable

**What happens.** Durvāsā, a sage, offers Indra a garland. Indra, preoccupied, drapes it on his elephant Airāvata, who tosses it to the ground and tramples it. Durvāsā curses the gods to lose their prosperity.

**Why it is in the film.** Opens the mythic frame with a small act of carelessness that becomes a catastrophe; pride and its price.

**The turn.** The elephant drops the garland and it is crushed.

**How it plays.** Indra’s court at full radiance: gold, pearl, music, every colour at its brightest. Durvāsā enters, unhurried, with a garland he means as a gift. Indra, half listening, takes it without looking and drapes it over Airāvata’s head. The great white elephant shakes it off and the garland falls under his foot. The court goes quiet. Durvāsā looks at the crushed flowers, then at Indra, who has still not understood what he has done. The sage speaks one sentence, a curse, and walks out through the light. The shot ends on the flowers.

**Source.** [SB 8.5](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/5/index.htm) — the full garland story is TRADITION (Viṣṇu Purāṇa)

### 2 · `E01-02` · The fall of the Devas
`1–2 min` · **drama** · CG tier 4 · Krishna: *Not present* · rasa *Karuṇa*  
**POV** Indra · **Place** Heaven; the field of battle; the gods’ flight · **Genre** Mythological epic, War, Tragedy

**What happens.** Prosperity drains from heaven. Colour fades from armour and court; strength leaves the Devas. The Asuras overrun them and the Devas flee as suppliants.

**Why it is in the film.** Sets up the world in need: the powerful become petitioners, the cost of pride.

**The turn.** Indra’s thunderbolt fails; the Devas run.

**How it plays.** The curse takes hold. Colour leaves the banners first, then the armour, then the faces. The Devas’ strength drains with it; Indra’s thunderbolt flickers and goes out. The Asuras come in from the edges, brighter than the gods now. The gods run. Some stop to help the fallen; most do not. The last image is the court, grey, with the garland’s crushed flowers still on the floor, and a long line of gods walking out of it.

**Source.** [SB 8.5](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/5/index.htm)

### 3 · `E01-03` · The petition to Brahmā and the Lord’s counsel
`2 min` · **drama** · CG tier 3 · Krishna: *Spoken of, indirect (voice only)* · rasa *Adbhuta*  
**POV** Indra · **Place** Brahmā’s realm; the shore of the Ocean of Milk · **Genre** Mythological epic, Spiritual drama, Political drama

**What happens.** The Devas go to Brahmā, who takes them to Viṣṇu. A voice answers: make an alliance with your enemies and churn the Ocean of Milk for amṛta; the Lord will arrange the rest.

**Why it is in the film.** Delivers the plan and the film’s grammar lesson: the Lord acts through others and is never in the room.

**The turn.** The voice says, in effect, “you will do it, and I will hold what you cannot.”

**How it plays.** The Devas go to Brahmā, who takes them across the sky to the shore of the Ocean of Milk. The water is still and white. They kneel. Nothing appears. A light moves across the surface, a voice speaks, and Brahmā alone hears it clearly. He turns to Indra and passes it on: make peace with your enemies, churn this ocean for nectar, and the rest will be held for you. The Devas look at one another. It is not what they wanted to hear. The camera stays on their faces.

**Source.** [SB 8.5](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/5/index.htm)

### 4 · `E01-04` · The humiliating alliance
`2 min` · **drama** · CG tier 2 · Krishna: *Spoken of, indirect* · rasa *Vīra*  
**POV** Indra · **Place** Asura camp; neutral ground · **Genre** Political drama, Mythological epic, Thriller

**What happens.** The Devas, proud and desperate, approach Bali and the Asuras and propose to churn the ocean together. Each side hides its own motive: the Devas want the nectar for themselves, the Asuras expect an equal share.

**Why it is in the film.** A pact of rivals that cannot hold; seeds the “seizing is not holding” theme.

**The turn.** Indra bows to ask Bali for help.

**How it plays.** Indra goes to Bali under a flag of truce. The two courts meet on neutral ground. Each side watches the other’s hands. Indra says the thing he did not want to say: they need each other. Bali listens. He knows exactly what the gods want and means to take his share anyway. A cup is passed. No one drinks. Both sides agree, each believing they have fooled the other.

**Source.** [SB 8.6](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/6/index.htm)

### 5 · `E01-05` · The mountain sinks; Kūrma lifts it
`2 min` · **action** · CG tier 5 · Krishna: *Effect* · rasa *Adbhuta*  
**POV** The Devas and Asuras (shared) · **Place** The shore and the Ocean of Milk · **Genre** Mythological epic, Disaster, Spectacle/wonder

**What happens.** Mandara is carried to the shore and used as the churning rod. It sinks. The two armies stare at the water. The mountain rises again, and the audience sees only a vast curved back beneath it: Kūrma, the tortoise.

**Why it is in the film.** The film’s clearest image of the unseen support: the Lord as landform under everything.

**The turn.** Something rises under the mountain.

**How it plays.** The mountain is brought to the shore and set upright in the ocean as the churning rod. It begins to sink. The two armies stare as it goes down under their rope. For a long moment there is only water. Then it rises, slowly, on something vast and curved beneath it. A shell-like back, ridged and wet, lifts the mountain clear. No one speaks. The armies pick up the rope.

**Source.** [SB 8.6](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/6/index.htm)

### 6 · `E01-06` · The great churning
`2 min` · **action** · CG tier 5 · Krishna: *Effect* · rasa *Vīra*  
**POV** The Devas at the tail · **Place** The Ocean of Milk · **Genre** Mythological epic, Adventure, Survival drama

**What happens.** The Devas hold the tail and the Asuras the head of Vāsuki. They pull in turn; the mountain spins, the sea convulses, the serpent breathes fire and smoke. Hands blister, strength fails, and something unseen empowers both sides.

**Why it is in the film.** Endurance as action; the long middle of the myth; the shared labour whose prize will not be shared.

**The turn.** The mountain keeps turning, as if lifted by a hand no one sees.

**How it plays.** The Devas hold the tail, the Asuras the head. They pull in turn. The mountain spins on the Lord’s back and the ocean turns with it. The serpent coughs smoke. Hands blister, backs bend, teeth set. A rhythm builds: pull, release, pull. Every strain is visible and no one sees what keeps the mountain from falling. A drop of nectar-white foam rises, and then another, and the sea begins to cloud. Both sides are exhausted. Neither lets go.

**Source.** [SB 8.6–8.7](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/6/index.htm)

### 7 · `E01-07` · Hālāhala rises
`1–2 min` · **action** · CG tier 4 · Krishna: *Not present* · rasa *Bhayānaka*  
**POV** The shore-watcher (fisher family) and the Devas · **Place** The Ocean of Milk · **Genre** Disaster, Horror, Mythological epic

**What happens.** The churning brings up the poison Hālāhala. It spreads on the water like black smoke. Beings flee in panic; fish die; the fisher child on the shore backs away from the water. Brahmā and the Devas call to Śiva.

**Why it is in the film.** The cost of the prize arrives first: before the gift, the poison.

**The turn.** A single fisher child stays at the water’s edge.

**How it plays.** The churning brings something up that is not nectar. A black stain spreads over the milk-white water. It moves faster than a man can run. Fish turn white and float. The Devas and Asuras drop the rope and flee along the shore. At the edge of the water, a fisher child stays where she is, one hand held out toward the dark. Brahmā calls to Śiva. No one else moves toward it.

**Source.** [SB 8.7](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/7/index.htm)

### 8 · `E01-08` · Śiva drinks the poison
`2 min` · **drama** · CG tier 3 · Krishna: *Not present* · rasa *Karuṇa*  
**POV** Pārvatī or the fisher child · **Place** The shore of the Ocean of Milk · **Genre** Spiritual drama, Mythological epic, Drama

**What happens.** Śiva walks toward the poison, cups it in his hand and drinks. Pārvatī’s hand rises to his throat to hold it there, and his throat turns blue. The Devas and Asuras stand silent. The churning resumes.

**Why it is in the film.** The first great act of absorption: one person walks into the harm so everyone else can live. Mirrors later acts by Krishna and Rukmini.

**The turn.** Pārvatī’s hand closes on his throat.

**How it plays.** Śiva walks toward the poison while everyone else runs from it. He kneels, cups it in one hand and lifts it. He drinks. Pārvatī’s hand rises to his throat, and holds. The skin there turns blue. For a long moment nothing else moves. Then the water begins to clear, a little at a time. The fisher child sees it. Behind her, someone starts to pull the rope again.

**Source.** [SB 8.7](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/7/index.htm)

### 9 · `E01-09` · The treasures rise
`2 min` · **drama** · CG tier 3 · Krishna: *Hands* · rasa *Adbhuta*  
**POV** The Devas and Asuras · **Place** The Ocean of Milk · **Genre** Mythological epic, Fantasy, Wonder/spectacle

**What happens.** With the poison held, the treasures rise: Surabhi the cow, the horse Uccaiḥśravā, the elephant Airāvata, the Kaustubha jewel, the Pārijāta tree, the Apsarās. Each is claimed. The jewel goes to the Lord, though no one sees the hand.

**Why it is in the film.** A scene of wonder and greed; each claim shows who the claimant is. Plants the Pārijāta tree.

**The turn.** The jewel is taken by an unseen hand and no one protests.

**How it plays.** With the poison held, the sea gives gifts. A cow rises, then a white horse, then an elephant like Airāvata, then a jewel that catches the sun. A tree comes up with flowers already open. Apsarās step from the foam. The Devas and Asuras claim them. A hand at the edge of the frame takes the jewel and no one objects. Nobody remarks on whose hand it was.

**Source.** [SB 8.8](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/8/index.htm)

### 10 · `E01-10` · Lakṣmī chooses
`2–3 min` · **drama** · CG tier 3 · Krishna: *Hands* · rasa *Śṛṅgāra*  
**POV** Lakṣmī · **Place** The shore; the assembled claimants · **Genre** Romance, Mythological epic, Drama

**What happens.** Lakṣmī emerges from the ocean with a garland. Gods, sages and Asuras stand ready, each certain she will choose them. She looks at each and passes on. At the end she walks to the one who has not stepped forward and places the garland on him; he holds her to his chest for good. The Asuras are left empty-handed.

**Why it is in the film.** The mythic mirror of Rukmini’s choice; the theme in one image: she chooses, she is not taken.

**The turn.** She passes every claimant and goes to the one who did not ask.

**How it plays.** Lakṣmī rises from the sea with a garland in her hands. The gods and the Asuras stand in a long arc, each sure it will be him. She walks along the line. She looks at each face for a moment and moves on. She passes the powerful and the proud and the handsome. At the end of the line she stops in front of the one who has not stepped forward, and she places the garland on him. The shot cuts so that we see only the garland settle on a shoulder. Behind her, the line goes quiet.

**Source.** [SB 8.8](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/8/index.htm) — the Lakṣmī–Rukmini identification is TRADITION

### 11 · `E01-11` · The Asuras refused
`1 min` · **drama** · CG tier 2 · Krishna: *Spoken of, indirect* · rasa *Raudra*  
**POV** Bali · **Place** The shore · **Genre** Drama, Political drama, Mythological epic

**What happens.** The Asuras stand where Lakṣmī passed them. Bali and his captains feel the refusal. Vāruṇī, the wine goddess, rises from the sea and Viṣṇu gives her to the Asuras as a consolation. They accept her.

**Why it is in the film.** Shows the rejected side as human; sets up the rage that will break the alliance.

**The turn.** They take Vāruṇī and swallow their anger.

**How it plays.** The Asuras stand where Lakṣmī passed them. Bali and his captains keep their faces still. The line shifts, and someone laughs. The wine goddess rises from the sea, and the Lord’s permission comes from offstage. The Asuras accept her. They take the cup and swallow their anger. They know exactly what they have lost.

**Source.** [SB 8.8](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/8/index.htm)

### 12 · `E01-12` · Dhanvantari and the seizure of the pot
`1–2 min` · **action** · CG tier 3 · Krishna: *Not present* · rasa *Raudra*  
**POV** Dhanvantari, then the Asuras · **Place** The shore · **Genre** Caper/heist, Drama, Mythological epic

**What happens.** Dhanvantari rises from the ocean carrying the pot of amṛta. The Asuras seize it in a rush. They begin to fight each other over who drinks first.

**Why it is in the film.** Greed turns winners against each other the moment they hold the prize: “seizing is not holding”.

**The turn.** The pot passes between hands and the circle breaks.

**How it plays.** Dhanvantari rises from the water holding a pot, and the Asuras go for it before he can say a word. Hands close on the handles. The pot passes from one to the next. Someone shouts that he saw it first. Someone else answers with a blow. The circle that held for the whole churning breaks. The gods watch from the edge, unmoving.

**Source.** [SB 8.8](https://vedabase.net/sb/8/8/index.htm)
