# The action, scene by scene

Every action beat in this story, elaborated one scene at a time: what happens
beat by beat, how the hero is kept off screen while it happens, what the unit
has to put in front of the camera, and the one shot the scene dies without.

Thirty-six scenes in all — the 22 on the board, the 6 written for the
commercial cut, and 8 that exist in the material and have never been written.

## How to read the tags

Every scene below carries one tag line in six fields. They are meant to be
typed into the board's tag field as they stand, so that the action can be
filtered by what it actually demands.

**KIND** — what sort of action it is:
`siege` · `mass battle` · `duel` · `chase` · `pursuit` · `breakout` ·
`escape` · `survival` · `scramble` (a fight over an object) · `labour`
(endurance, no opponent) · `deployment` (force shown, not used) ·
`execution` (force used in one stroke) · `disaster` · `montage` · `road`

**MOVE** — what carries the scene: `static` · `on foot` · `climb` · `horse` ·
`chariot` · `water` · `aerial`

**SCALE** — `solo` · `two-hander` · `squad` · `army` · `crowd` · `cosmic`

**HERO** — how Krishna appears, which before `E06-18` may only be:
`absent` · `sound` · `effect` · `hand` · `silhouette` · `voice` ·
`reflection`; and after it, `visible`

**UNIT** — what has to be arranged: `crowd` · `animals` · `fire` · `water` ·
`stunts` · `wire` · `chariot rig` · `VFX creature` · `VFX environment` ·
`practical only`

**RASA** — the register the scene is played in: `Vīra` (heroic) ·
`Bhayānaka` (fear) · `Raudra` (fury) · `Adbhuta` (wonder) · `Karuṇa` (grief)

---

## The law that shapes every fight in this film

**The hero cannot be seen before `E06-18`.** That is not a limitation on this
film's action, it is the action's style. Across the twenty-two action scenes on
the board he is `absent` in six, `sound` or `silhouette` in five, an `effect`
in five, a `hand` or a `voice` in three, and `visible` in two — both after the
reveal.

Four rules follow, and the audience has learned all four inside twenty minutes:

1. **The result is the shot, not the blow.** The women on the wall faint and
   wake to an empty plain.
2. **Somebody else does the killing.** The plough sweeps the infantry; a
   sleeper's glance burns the man who woke him; Balarama holds the ford.
3. **He is in frame as a consequence** — a conch, a hand at the edge, a
   silhouette against fire, a voice beside a frightened woman.
4. **He never wins by strength.** He outruns, outwaits, outthinks, or walks out
   unarmed and lets himself be chased.

# Part one · The twenty-two on the board

## SP1 · The churning
*Three scenes, ≈ 5.5 min. The only action sequence in the film with no enemy in
it: what is being fought is the work.*

### E01-05 · The mountain sinks; Kūrma lifts it
`2 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** labour · **MOVE** water · **SCALE** cosmic · **HERO** effect ·
**UNIT** crowd, VFX creature, VFX environment · **RASA** Adbhuta

**The beats.** (1) Mandara comes to the shore on the shoulders of two armies
who hate each other, and the hatred shows in how badly they carry it. (2) It is
set upright in the water as a churning rod, and for a moment it stands.
(3) It begins to go down — not dramatically, just steadily, the way something
too heavy goes down. (4) The rope slackens in a thousand hands. (5) Two armies
stand at the edge of the sea looking at flat water where a mountain was.
(6) The water domes. (7) The mountain comes back up, slowly, on something vast,
curved, ridged and wet, and the camera never gives the whole of it — a back
filling the bottom third of frame, and the mountain riding on it. (8) Nobody
speaks. Somebody picks up the rope. Then everybody does.

**Keeping him off screen.** He *is* the landform. The shot is composed so the
shell reads as geography — an island, a reef — and the audience only works out
what it was watching when the mountain starts turning in the next scene.

**What the unit needs.** A shoreline, two crowds in opposing palettes, one
practical rope run at scale, and the film's single most expensive creature
asset, used for about nine seconds.

**The shot that has to work.** The dome of water before the shell breaks it. If
the water reads as water, the shell reads as alive.

### E01-06 · The great churning
`2 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** labour · **MOVE** water · **SCALE** cosmic · **HERO** effect ·
**UNIT** crowd, VFX creature, VFX environment · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) Devas at the tail, Asuras at the head: the serpent Vāsuki
is the rope, and the first pull establishes that both ends are full of people
who would rather be pulling the other way. (2) Pull, release, pull — the
rhythm is built as a work rhythm, not a battle rhythm. (3) The mountain spins
and the ocean turns with it, a spiral of white opening out to the horizon.
(4) The serpent coughs smoke down the Asura line, and the Asura line keeps
pulling. (5) Close on hands: blisters, then split skin, then blood on the
scales. (6) Backs bend, teeth set, and an old Deva drops and is dragged aside
without the rhythm breaking. (7) A white foam rises; then another; the sea
begins to cloud. (8) Both sides are finished and neither lets go.

**Keeping him off screen.** The mountain does not fall, and nothing in frame
explains why. The camera goes under once, far enough to see the mountain's base
resting on something, and comes back up.

**What the unit needs.** The serpent (shared asset with E01-05's water sim),
a repeatable crowd choreography, and a prosthetic-hands pass for the close-ups.

**The shot that has to work.** The hands. Everything cosmic in this sequence is
paid for by two seconds of split skin.

### E01-07 · Hālāhala rises
`1–2 min · CG tier 4`
**KIND** disaster · **MOVE** water · **SCALE** cosmic · **HERO** absent ·
**UNIT** crowd, animals, VFX environment · **RASA** Bhayānaka

**The beats.** (1) The churning brings up something that is not nectar: a black
stain, first the size of a boat, on milk-white water. (2) It spreads faster
than a man can run — the one piece of information the scene has to deliver, and
it is delivered by a tracking shot that loses a race. (3) Fish turn white and
come up dead in a line that keeps extending. (4) Both armies drop the rope and
run along the shore, together, for the first and only time. (5) A heron goes up
off the water and comes down in it. (6) At the waterline a fisher child stays
where she is, one hand held out toward the dark. (7) Brahmā calls for Śiva, and
the call goes out over an empty beach. (8) Nobody else moves toward the water.

**Keeping him off screen.** He is nowhere in it. This is the scene that
establishes the film's bargain: when the worst thing happens, someone walks
into it — and it is not always him.

**What the unit needs.** Water surface work, a dead-fish practical pass, one
child, and a beach that can be emptied in one take.

**The shot that has to work.** The child's hand, held out, with the stain
coming. It pays off twice — in [E01-08], and three hours later in [E01-17].

## SP2 · The pot
*One scene, ≈ 1.5 min. A fight over an object, which the film uses as its
thesis.*

### E01-12 · Dhanvantari and the seizure of the pot
`1–2 min · CG tier 3`
**KIND** scramble · **MOVE** on foot · **SCALE** crowd · **HERO** absent ·
**UNIT** crowd, stunts · **RASA** Raudra

**The beats.** (1) Dhanvantari comes up out of the water holding a pot, and the
shot is clean and ceremonial for about two seconds. (2) The Asuras take it
before he can speak — not a charge, a grab, with the ugly closeness of a
crowd at a door. (3) Hands close on the handles; the pot goes from one to the
next above the heads. (4) Somebody shouts that he saw it first. (5) Somebody
answers with a blow, and the sound of the blow is the loudest thing in the
scene. (6) The circle that held through the entire churning comes apart in
eleven seconds. (7) The gods watch from the edge of frame and do not move.
(8) The pot is still above the heads, going nowhere.

**Keeping him off screen.** Absent. The scene's argument — that holding is
harder than taking — is made by the people who took it.

**What the unit needs.** One practical pot, a tight crowd, and stunt grabs. No
VFX beyond the water exit.

**The shot that has to work.** The pot passing hand to hand above the heads
while the fight starts underneath it.

## SP3 · The Deva–Asura war
*One scene, ≈ 2 min. The only conventional battle in the film, fought entirely
by other people.*

### E01-15 · The Deva–Asura war
`2 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** mass battle · **MOVE** on foot, aerial · **SCALE** cosmic ·
**HERO** absent · **UNIT** crowd, VFX environment, wire · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) The alliance ends on a look, not a line. (2) The Asuras open
with illusions: fire that gives no heat, a second army that vanishes when
struck, ground that is not there. (3) Bali leads from the front, which is the
scene's one piece of characterisation — he is the better soldier of the two
commanders. (4) The Devas lose ground and keep losing it, long enough for the
audience to believe it. (5) Indra answers with the thunderbolt and the whole
field flashes white. (6) Nectar-strength takes hold: blows that were landing
without effect start landing. (7) The line turns. (8) Colour comes back across
the field banner by banner — the same colour that drained out in [E01-02] —
and heaven has its light again.

**Keeping him off screen.** Absent. The war he arranged is won without him in
it, which is exactly the shape of the Mathurā siege eighty minutes later.

**What the unit needs.** The film's largest crowd, a flash-frame grade, and an
illusion-VFX package that can be reused for nothing else.

**The shot that has to work.** Colour returning across the field. It is the
only way the audience knows who won.

## SP4 · The siege arrives
*One scene, ≈ 1.5 min. Scale measured by one man's inability to count it.*

### E03-02 · Twenty-three divisions at the gate
`1–2 min · CG tier 4`
**KIND** deployment · **MOVE** static · **SCALE** army · **HERO** absent ·
**UNIT** crowd, VFX environment · **RASA** Bhayānaka

**The beats.** (1) Morning: a line of dust on the horizon, which could be
weather. (2) Midday: tents. (3) Afternoon: banners, enough of them that the
water-carrier puts his bucket down. (4) He starts counting divisions aloud,
gets to nine, loses his place, starts again, and gives up. (5) Evening: a
country of cooking fires reaching to every horizon, and the city's own lamps
suddenly look small. (6) The sound mix changes — it is the first time Mathurā
has an outside noise. (7) He takes a piece of chalk and makes one mark on the
wall, because there is nothing else a man with a bucket can do. (8) Hold on the
single mark.

**Keeping him off screen.** Absent. The film introduces its war with a civilian
and a piece of chalk, and never gives the enemy a hero shot.

**What the unit needs.** One wall-top location in four light conditions, a
matte-extended camp, and one actor.

**The shot that has to work.** The first chalk mark, framed so that seventeen
of them will fit.

## SP5 · The conch, the battle, and the release
*Three scenes, ≈ 6.5 min. The film's model battle: sound before sight,
witnesses instead of coverage, and a moral turn that costs the victory its
point.*

### E03-04 · The sky-chariots and the conch
`2 min · CG tier 4`
**KIND** deployment · **MOVE** chariot, aerial · **SCALE** army ·
**HERO** sound · **UNIT** chariot rig, animals, VFX environment ·
**RASA** Adbhuta

**The beats.** (1) The plain outside Mathurā, both armies in position, nothing
happening. (2) A column of light comes down and two chariots come down inside
it, complete with drivers and racked weapons — the audience gets a good look at
the chariots and no look at all at the men. (3) Two figures step up into them
from behind, in one unbroken shot that holds on the wheels. (4) The gate opens
and a small column rides out, and the word *small* is the point: the camera
pulls wide enough to show how few. (5) Silence. (6) A conch sounds, long and
clear, held past comfort. (7) Jarasandha's front line steps backward — a whole
rank, involuntarily, before a single weapon has moved. (8) Jarasandha looks at
his own front line stepping back.

**Keeping him off screen.** `sound`. He is a conch and a pair of feet on a
chariot board. The enemy's reaction is the performance.

**What the unit needs.** Two hero chariots, a light-column plate, horses, and a
crowd that can step back on cue as one body.

**The shot that has to work.** The front rank stepping back. The film's whole
economy — power shown through other people's bodies — is proved or lost there.

### E03-06 · The battle seen from the wall
`3 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** mass battle · **MOVE** chariot · **SCALE** army · **HERO** effect ·
**UNIT** crowd, animals, chariot rig, VFX environment · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) The enemy closes on the two chariots, and the camera is on
the wall, with the women. (2) The chariots disappear behind dust and men — the
audience loses them at the same moment the women do. (3) The women watch.
(4) One faints. Then another. The scene's clock is now the women going down.
(5) On the field, in glimpses only: arrows taking elephants off their feet,
horses turning over, chariots coming apart. (6) The plough sweeps a line of
foot soldiers aside and the shot does not show who is holding it. (7) The dust
thins. (8) The women wake, one at a time, to a plain empty of the enemy and two
chariots standing exactly where they stood.

**Keeping him off screen.** `effect`. There is no shot of him fighting at all —
the battle is a gap in the middle of the scene, and the gap is the frightening
part.

**What the unit needs.** The wall set with its gallery, fifteen or twenty
actresses who can faint convincingly in sequence, and a field pass that can be
glimpsed rather than held.

**The shot that has to work.** The wake-up. Four women opening their eyes on an
empty plain, in one take.

### E03-07 · Jarasandha seized and freed
`1–2 min · CG tier 2`
**KIND** disarm · **MOVE** static · **SCALE** two-hander · **HERO** hand ·
**UNIT** practical only · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) The field after. Balarama has Jarasandha by the throat and a
rope in his other hand. (2) He starts to bind him, and he is enjoying it.
(3) A hand rises at the edge of frame. (4) A word follows it — one word, not
a speech. (5) Balarama stops. (6) He looks at the rope, then at the hand, then
at the rope. (7) His fist opens, and Jarasandha stumbles back and runs for his
life without dignity. (8) Balarama watches him go with something close to
disgust, and the disgust is at his brother.

**Keeping him off screen.** `hand`. The cheapest shot in the film and the one
that does the most work: a hand and a word reverse a complete victory.

**What the unit needs.** Three actors, a rope, a field. No VFX, no stunts.

**The shot that has to work.** The hand entering frame. It must be ordinary —
no light on it, no music sting — because the film is saving the face for
minute 171.

## SP6 · Seventeen
*One scene, ≈ 2 min. Seventeen years of war in one wall, and the cheapest
action in the film.*

### E03-09 · Seventeen times
`2 min · CG tier 4`
**KIND** montage, siege · **MOVE** static · **SCALE** army · **HERO** effect ·
**UNIT** crowd, fire, animals, water, VFX environment · **RASA** Karuṇa

**The beats.** (1) A night assault: torches coming up a ladder, gone by
morning. Chalk. (2) A fire assault: burning carts rolled at a gate. Chalk.
(3) An elephant charge against the same gate. Chalk. (4) Siege towers, built
patiently over a season. Chalk. (5) A flood — a river turned at the lower town.
Chalk. (6) Between each one, the same two shots: the water-carrier's bucket
going down, and the king being let go. (7) The wall grows seventeen strokes
long, and the strokes are getting shakier because the hand is getting older.
(8) On the seventeenth he steps back, and there is no army behind it.

**Keeping him off screen.** `effect`. Every attempt is shown from the wall and
every outcome is a chalk mark. He appears exactly as often as the audience
believes: not at all.

**What the unit needs.** One wall, five short second-unit passes, and ageing
makeup on one actor. Any of the five passes can be dropped without breaking the
scene, which is why this is the most robust action scene in the film.

**The shot that has to work.** The seventeenth mark with nothing behind it.

## SP7 · The chase on foot and the burning hill
*Three scenes, ≈ 5 min. The only sequence in which either brother is afraid,
and the only one shot as survival rather than war.*

### E03-11 · The chase on foot
`2 min · CG tier 3`
**KIND** escape · **MOVE** on foot · **SCALE** army · **HERO** silhouette ·
**UNIT** crowd, animals, stunts · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) Jarasandha comes back with twenty-three divisions while the
Yadavas are moving wealth to the coast — the one time the enemy's timing is
good. (2) Two men leave the road. They do not ride; they run. (3) The king
rides behind them, laughing, with a host filling the road behind him. (4) Cut
rhythm: the two men get smaller, the host does not get smaller. (5) They cross
broken ground where horses have to slow, which is the only reason they are
alive. (6) Somebody in the host shouts that they are going to the hills.
(7) They turn off the plain. (8) They begin to climb.

**Keeping him off screen.** `silhouette`, at distance, always from the
pursuer's eye. The audience is riding with Jarasandha for this scene.

**What the unit needs.** A long road location, mounted crowd, and two doubles
who can run a ridge.

**The shot that has to work.** The size relationship: two figures against a
road full of cavalry, held long enough to be hopeless.

### E03-12 · The burning hill
`2 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** survival · **MOVE** climb · **SCALE** two-hander ·
**HERO** silhouette · **UNIT** fire, stunts, VFX environment ·
**RASA** Bhayānaka

**The beats.** (1) The hill is called Pravarṣaṇa, and they go up it because
there is nowhere else to go. (2) Below, the king cannot find them, and that is
the moment he decides to stop looking. (3) Fire is set at the base in a
ring — an administrative act, done by men with torches walking apart from each
other. (4) Fire runs up through the trees faster than two men can climb.
(5) They climb above the smoke, and the view opens, and it is beautiful, which
makes it worse. (6) They run out of mountain. (7) Balarama looks down at the
drop, then across at his brother. (8) Krishna is a figure in the smoke with his
back to the fire, and for the first time in the film they are both afraid.

**Keeping him off screen.** `silhouette`. The smoke does the work the darkness
does elsewhere; his back is to the fire, so he is an outline inside an orange
field.

**What the unit needs.** A controlled-burn hillside or a full environment
build, two stunt doubles on a ridge, and the film's one serious fire unit day.

**The shot that has to work.** The two outlines on the last ten feet of rock
with nothing above them.

### E03-13 · The leap and the false victory
`1–2 min · CG tier 3`
**KIND** escape · **MOVE** aerial · **SCALE** two-hander · **HERO** effect ·
**UNIT** wire, fire, stunts · **RASA** Adbhuta

**The beats.** (1) They jump. (2) The shot follows one silhouette down against
the flames — and stops before the ground. (3) The fire rises into the frame
they left. (4) At the bottom of the hill, Jarasandha waits for bodies, and he
waits long enough to look foolish. (5) A commander comes back and reports no
sign of them in the ash. (6) He turns it over, and decides he has won. (7) He
goes home to Magadha, satisfied, and his army is relieved to be going.
(8) On the hill, the fire burns itself out.

**Keeping him off screen.** `effect`. The audience is denied the landing
exactly as the enemy is denied the bodies — the only time the film puts the
audience and the antagonist in the same position.

**What the unit needs.** One wire descent, a fire plate, and an ash field.

**The shot that has to work.** The cut away before the ground. If the film
shows the landing, it has told the audience they are safe, and the next hour
costs nothing.

## SP8 · The unarmed chase and the ash
*Three scenes, ≈ 4.5 min. The best action idea in the film: the hunted chooses
the route, and the kill belongs to somebody else's reflex.*

### E05-03 · The unarmed chase
`2–3 min · CG tier 4`
**KIND** chase · **MOVE** on foot · **SCALE** two-hander (army falling away) ·
**HERO** silhouette · **UNIT** stunts, crowd, VFX environment ·
**RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) Kālayavana runs, and he is fast, and he does not catch him.
(2) The man ahead is always one step out of reach — never further, which is
the detail that makes it a trap instead of a chase. (3) A field: the army is
still behind them. (4) A river: half the army stops at the water. (5) A ridge:
the army is gone, and it is two men in empty country. (6) Kālayavana starts
shouting — *coward, runaway, one who will not fight* — and the shouting is how
the audience measures the distance. (7) The man does not turn, and the film
lets the insult stand unanswered, which is the hardest thing it asks of its
hero. (8) The hills close in. Ahead, a dark opening in the rock.

**Keeping him off screen.** `silhouette`, from behind, at the same distance for
the whole scene. The audience spends three minutes looking at the back of a
head and never gets more.

**What the unit needs.** Three progressively wilder locations, a stunt runner
who can hold an exact gap, and one lead who can carry a scene with his back.

**The shot that has to work.** The constant distance. If it varies, the scene
becomes an ordinary chase and the trap stops being a trap.

### E05-05 · The kick and the ash
`1 min · CG tier 3`
**KIND** execution · **MOVE** static · **SCALE** two-hander ·
**HERO** absent · **UNIT** VFX creature light, practical ash ·
**RASA** Raudra

**The beats.** (1) The cave. A man asleep on the floor, lit by what comes
through the mouth. (2) Kālayavana decides this is Krishna pretending, and the
decision is visible on his face — arrogance mistaking stillness for a trick.
(3) He raises his foot. (4) The foot lands. (5) Mucukunda's eyes open. (6) Half
a second of nothing — the scene's only beat of silence, and it must be long
enough to be uncomfortable. (7) The light out of his eyes fills the cave.
(8) When it clears there is a shape of ash on the floor, and behind a rock,
somebody waits.

**Keeping him off screen.** `absent`. The biggest kill in the film and he is
not in the shot. He is a man waiting behind a rock for something he arranged.

**What the unit needs.** A cave set, an interactive-light rig, and an ash
dressing. Under a day.

**The shot that has to work.** The half-second of nothing after the eyes open.

### E05-12 · The Yavana army destroyed
`1 min · CG tier 3`
**KIND** siege · **MOVE** static · **SCALE** army · **HERO** effect ·
**UNIT** crowd, VFX environment · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) The gate of Mathurā opens. (2) Outside, the plain is full of
the Yavana army. (3) Cut. The same frame, nearly empty. (4) Wreckage, and
silence where there was an army's noise. (5) Carts are loaded with the dead
army's wealth. (6) The carts take the road toward the sea. (7) The
water-carrier on the wall looks at his seventeen chalk marks. (8) He chalks a
line under the last of them.

**Keeping him off screen.** `effect`, in two shots. The film's most aggressive
use of the cut: thirty million men are removed in a single edit.

**What the unit needs.** One locked-off frame shot twice, dressed full and
dressed empty.

**The shot that has to work.** The match. The two frames must be identical or
the edit is just a transition.

## SP9 · Two rides
*Two scenes, ≈ 4 min. Action with no opponent: two men carrying a piece of
paper against a clock.*

### E06-05 · The Brahmin's ride
`2 min · CG tier 3`
**KIND** road · **MOVE** on foot · **SCALE** solo · **HERO** absent ·
**UNIT** water, animals, practical · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) He leaves before dawn with the letter inside his clothes,
and touches it twice before he is out of the gate. (2) A guard post: a soldier
asks his business and he says something about a pilgrimage, and the lie is
clumsy and works. (3) A river, up and brown, and a ferryman who will not cross
until he is paid double — so he pays it, and the audience learns he has no
money left. (4) A rider appears on the road behind him. (5) He walks faster.
(6) Then slower, deliberately, and stands aside with his head down, and the
rider goes by without looking. (7) At the next village he takes the letter out.
(8) It is dry.

**Keeping him off screen.** `absent`. A whole action scene about him in which
he does not exist yet.

**What the unit needs.** A road, a river crossing, one horse, four small parts.
The cheapest scene in this file.

**The shot that has to work.** The letter coming out dry. It is the only
"victory" in the sequence and it has to land like one.

### E06-07 · The night ride
`2 min · CG tier 3`
**KIND** road · **MOVE** chariot · **SCALE** two-hander · **HERO** sound ·
**UNIT** chariot rig, animals, VFX environment · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) He reads the hour of the wedding and calls for the chariot,
and does not wait to be asked twice — the whole decision is one gesture.
(2) Dāruka brings it, already running. (3) The Brahmin climbs in, holding on
badly. (4) They leave in the dark. (5) The road goes under the wheels, and the
sound is hooves and wheel-rim and nothing else. (6) The stars turn over — one
long lens, one dissolve, the only overtly beautiful shot in the film's action.
(7) Somewhere in the night the Brahmin closes his eyes, because he is old and
it has been a long week. (8) He opens them on a grey line in the east and the
plain of Vidarbha.

**Keeping him off screen.** `sound`. Reins, a shoulder, a voice giving one
order. In the commercial cut this scene is the interval.

**What the unit needs.** A chariot rig, a night unit, a star plate, and a road
that can be shot in both directions.

**The shot that has to work.** Dawn arriving on the plain. It has to feel like
relief and like a deadline at the same time.

## SP10 · The breakout
*Two scenes, ≈ 2.5 min. The only action in the film where he is visible, and
she is still the one the camera is on.*

### E06-19 · The lift
`1 min · CG tier 4`
**KIND** breakout · **MOVE** chariot · **SCALE** crowd · **HERO** visible ·
**UNIT** crowd, chariot rig, stunts · **RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) She comes through the forecourt and he is already moving.
(2) He does not wait for her to climb — he takes her up in front of every king
in India. (3) Her foot is already on the board, because she decided this a week
ago; the scene plays as cooperation, not capture. (4) The chariot jolts.
(5) Dāruka's hands are on the reins before anybody's sword is out. (6) For half
a second nobody in the forecourt understands what has happened. (7) She turns
and looks at the faces behind her — her father's, her brother's, the
bridegroom's. (8) She does not look back at the temple.

**Keeping him off screen.** `visible`. Eighty seconds after the reveal, and the
camera is still on her: his face is in the frame, hers is the subject of it.

**What the unit needs.** The forecourt crowd, a chariot that can start hard,
and one lift rehearsed to the frame.

**The shot that has to work.** Her foot already on the board. It is the
difference between this film and every other version of this story.

### E06-20 · Through the ring of kings
`1–2 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** breakout · **MOVE** chariot · **SCALE** army · **HERO** silhouette ·
**UNIT** crowd, animals, chariot rig, stunts, VFX environment ·
**RASA** Vīra

**The beats.** (1) The chariot goes at the ring and does not slow down.
(2) Balarama's men open the way in front of it, and the opening is only just
wide enough. (3) The conch sounds, long and clear — the same note as
[E03-04], which is the audience's cue that this is a battle and not an
elopement. (4) Kings draw swords too late, in ones and twos, and look foolish
doing it. (5) She holds the rail and does not look down. (6) The road opens
ahead. (7) Behind her, a hundred horses turn at once — one aerial, the only one
the film needs. (8) Dust closes over the temple.

**Keeping him off screen.** `silhouette` again, oddly: he is driving, lit from
in front, and the scene is composed on her face and on the kings' faces.

**What the unit needs.** The film's biggest practical crowd, a chariot running
at speed through it, mounted stunts, and one aerial plate.

**The shot that has to work.** The hundred horses turning together behind her.

## SP11 · The pursuit
*One scene, ≈ 2.5 min. The only battle in the film with a romantic lead inside
it.*

### E06-22 · The pursuit and the Yadava defence
`2–3 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** pursuit, mass battle · **MOVE** chariot, horse · **SCALE** army ·
**HERO** voice · **UNIT** crowd, animals, chariot rig, stunts ·
**RASA** Bhayānaka

**The beats.** (1) The kings arm, and the arming is fast and competent — they
are not fools, they are humiliated professionals. (2) The chase goes onto open
plain, which is the worst possible ground for the people being chased.
(3) Behind the chariot the Yadava commanders turn and face them, which means
they have decided to die there. (4) Arrows fall like rain. (5) She watches them
land and thinks, for the first time in the film, about the men who are dying
for a letter she wrote. (6) Beside her, quietly, a voice says that her side
will finish them shortly — the only thing he says during any action in the
film. (7) She looks back. (8) Horses go down, elephants turn, and the chase
comes apart in pieces.

**Keeping him off screen.** `voice`. The scene is shot over her shoulder; he is
a presence at the edge of the frame with one line.

**What the unit needs.** Mounted crowd on open ground, an arrow-fall pass, and
a chariot that can carry a two-person dialogue scene at speed.

**The shot that has to work.** Her face while the arrows land. The battle is
background; her realisation is the scene.

## SP12 · The duel
*One scene, ≈ 3 min. The climax, and it is a disarming rather than a fight.*

### E06-25 · The duel
`3 min · CG tier 5`
**KIND** duel · **MOVE** chariot, on foot · **SCALE** two-hander ·
**HERO** effect, face · **UNIT** stunts, chariot rig, practical weapons ·
**RASA** Bhayānaka

**The beats.** (1) Rukmī catches up alone, having sworn an oath in front of
everybody he knows. (2) He attacks, and his bow breaks. (3) He takes another.
It breaks. (4) A third — and now the pattern is the scene: weapon after weapon,
shield after shield, nothing landing, nothing even getting close. (5) The
humiliation builds past anger into something closer to panic. (6) He jumps down
with a sword, which is the first honest thing he does in the film. (7) The
sword is cut to pieces. (8) The other sword turns, and she is watching from the
chariot while the two men she loves move toward the end of it.

**Keeping him off screen.** `effect` and, now, a face — but the coverage still
favours Rukmī and her. The audience watches a man lose rather than a man win.

**What the unit needs.** Breakaway bows and shields in quantity, a two-man
fight unit, and a chariot platform for her coverage.

**The shot that has to work.** The third bow breaking. Once the audience sees
the pattern, the rest of the duel is dread rather than action — which is what
makes [E06-26] possible.

# Part two · The six written for the commercial cut

Specified in `COMMERCIAL_CUT.md`; not on the board. ≈ 17 min.

### NEW-01 · Out of the smoke
`2.5 min · tier 4`
**KIND** escape · **MOVE** on foot · **SCALE** two-hander ·
**HERO** visible *(commercial cut only)* · **UNIT** fire, stunts ·
**RASA** Adbhuta

Two men walk down off a burnt hill with the fire still going behind them —
ash on the shoulders, no weapons, no hurry. The commercial cut's entry, and the
only time that version of the film announces him: the camera holds on his face,
which the master cut saves for minute 171. No line until the last beat.

### NEW-02 · The road back, and the city that sees them
`2 min · tier 3`
**KIND** road · **MOVE** on foot, horse · **SCALE** crowd · **HERO** visible ·
**UNIT** crowd, animals · **RASA** Vīra

The news outruns them: a cart driver who stares and turns his cart around, a
gate that opens before they reach it, a street that fills from both ends.
Establishes the man as a rumour before he is a king — the master cut's entire
method, compressed into two minutes of elevation.

### NEW-07 · Jarasandha deploys: the ring around Kuṇḍina
`2.5 min · tier 4`
**KIND** deployment · **MOVE** horse · **SCALE** army · **HERO** absent ·
**UNIT** crowd, animals, VFX environment · **RASA** Bhayānaka

The coalition as a map: who holds which road, how many on each, and the one
road nobody is watching. Shot as a briefing crossed with a muster, so that the
chase twenty minutes later has rules the audience already knows.

### NEW-08 · The chariot through the market
`3.5 min · tier 4`
**KIND** breakout · **MOVE** chariot · **SCALE** crowd · **HERO** visible ·
**UNIT** crowd, stunts, chariot rig, animals · **RASA** Vīra

A street built that morning for a wedding, now a corridor at speed: garlands
coming down over the horses, an overturned cart, a cloth stall going up like a
sail, a child pulled out of the way by a stranger. The one action scene in
either cut that is pure chase pleasure, with no argument attached.

### NEW-09 · Balarama holds the ford
`4 min · tier 4`
**KIND** last stand · **MOVE** water, on foot · **SCALE** squad against army ·
**HERO** absent · **UNIT** water, stunts, crowd, animals · **RASA** Vīra

One man and a plough-blade at a river crossing, buying the chariot an hour. The
film's biggest fight and the hero is not in it — which is the point, and why
this scene is the clearest statement of the whole film inside the commercial
cut.

### NEW-10 · Śiśupāla turns back
`2.5 min · tier 3`
**KIND** pursuit · **MOVE** horse · **SCALE** solo · **HERO** absent ·
**UNIT** animals, stunts · **RASA** Karuṇa

The bridegroom rides at the chariot alone, in wedding clothes, and is simply
passed over — not fought, not disarmed, not acknowledged. He turns back unhurt
and unlooked-at, which is worse than a wound and is the seed of everything he
does afterwards.

# Part three · Eight that have never been written

Each is one line, one image or one implication inside an existing scene.

### A1 · The flood
`≈ 3 min · tier 5 · opens one chalk mark in [E03-09]`
**KIND** disaster, siege · **MOVE** water · **SCALE** crowd ·
**HERO** effect · **UNIT** water, crowd, VFX environment · **RASA** Bhayānaka

Jarasandha's cleverest attempt and currently a single mark on a wall. Men
breaking a bank upstream by lamplight; water arriving in the lower town at
night, ankle-deep, then knee-deep, then carrying things; the water-carrier's own
trade turned into the weapon against him; a street of people on roofs. It ends
the way all seventeen end, but it is the only one where the city nearly goes.
**The best single addition available to this film's action.**

### A2 · The night assault · the fire carts · the elephants · the towers
`≈ 2–3 min each · tier 4 · the other four marks in [E03-09]`
**KIND** siege · **MOVE** on foot / animals / static · **SCALE** army ·
**HERO** effect · **UNIT** fire, animals, crowd · **RASA** Vīra

Each of the remaining four marks is a complete set piece sitting inside one
line of montage. **Open at most one.** Two, and the montage stops making its
argument — that the enemy's inventiveness is irrelevant.

### A3 · Two gates, two armies
`≈ 4 min · tier 4 · expands [E04-01] and [E04-02]`
**KIND** deployment · **MOVE** on foot (walk along a wall) · **SCALE** army ·
**HERO** absent · **UNIT** crowd, VFX environment · **RASA** Bhayānaka

Thirty million men at one gate while twenty-three divisions are still camped at
the other. Currently a one-minute image and a council scene. As action it is
the film's only real *trap*, and it should be shot as a single walk along the
walls from one horizon to the other, with the camera turning once.

### A4 · The rider on the Brahmin's road
`≈ 2 min · tier 2 · expands [E06-05]`
**KIND** chase · **MOVE** on foot, horse · **SCALE** two-hander ·
**HERO** absent · **UNIT** animals, practical · **RASA** Bhayānaka

Rukmī sends a man after the letter. In the scene as written, the rider goes
past. Opened up it is the cheapest action in the film — one road, two men, no
army — and it puts the only object that matters in the whole picture inside a
chase.

### A5 · The ford, extended
`≈ 8 min · tier 4 · expands [NEW-09]`
**KIND** last stand · **MOVE** water · **SCALE** squad against army ·
**HERO** absent · **UNIT** water, stunts, crowd, animals · **RASA** Vīra

Four minutes could carry eight, and this is the right place to spend them: a
crossing held in three waves, with the hero off screen for all of it, so the
longest action sequence in the film is the audience watching somebody else pay
for his decision.

### A6 · The arena at Mathurā
`≈ 6 min · tier 4 · only if C1 Kaṁsa is built (see CANDIDATES.md)`
**KIND** mass battle, execution · **MOVE** on foot · **SCALE** crowd ·
**HERO** effect · **UNIT** crowd, animals, stunts · **RASA** Raudra

A bow broken in a public street, an elephant turned loose in a gateway, two
wrestlers in a ring, and a king coming off his own dais onto his own floor — all
of it shot from inside the crowd, which is where this film shoots battles
anyway.

### A7 · Pauṇḍraka's duel, and the head that goes home
`≈ 4 min · tier 4 · only if C3 Pauṇḍraka is built`
**KIND** duel · **MOVE** chariot · **SCALE** two-hander · **HERO** effect ·
**UNIT** stunts, chariot rig, VFX light · **RASA** Hāsya turning to Raudra

A man who has had the marks made, fighting with borrowed insignia and losing to
his own weapons, and his head arriving back in the city that had been calling
him Vāsudeva. **The only fight in the film that can be shot conventionally**,
because the visible man in it is the fake.

### A8 · The muster at Magadha
`≈ 3 min · tier 3 · only if C6 Magadha is built`
**KIND** deployment · **MOVE** static · **SCALE** army · **HERO** absent ·
**UNIT** crowd, practical · **RASA** Karuṇa

Not a battle: an army being assembled for the seventeenth time. A quartermaster
who has done this sixteen times and knows exactly how many carts it takes; men
who are younger in every muster; a king watching from a window. Action as
dread.

# Part four · The grammar

Eight devices, each already used somewhere above, each reusable anywhere.

| device | where | what it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sound before sight** | the conch, [E03-04] and [E06-20] | The enemy reacts before the audience sees anything. The cheapest power in the film. |
| **The witness who faints** | the wall, [E03-06] | Cuts the middle out of a battle and makes the gap frightening instead of cheap. |
| **Cut to the result** | [E03-06], [E05-12] | Two identical frames, full and empty, do what four minutes of coverage would do worse. |
| **Somebody else's hands** | the plough [E03-06], the glance [E05-05], the ford [NEW-09] | The biggest kills belong to other people, which is the film's argument. |
| **The hand at the edge of frame** | [E03-07] | One hand and one word reverse a complete victory. |
| **The silhouette against fire** | [E03-12], [E03-13] | The only way he is ever shown in danger, and the shot stops before the ground. |
| **The voice beside her** | [E06-22] | Turns a battle into a two-hander by putting the reassurance inside the noise. |
| **The hunted leading** | [E05-03] | A chase in which the man being chased chooses the route. Patience as action. |

Four things the action must never do:

- **No clear view of him landing a blow before [E06-18]** — not in shadow, not
  in a wide, not for one frame.
- **No slow motion on him before the reveal**, and when the reveal comes it is
  her eyes finding him, not a hero shot.
- **No fight won by being stronger.** Every win is patience, speed, foresight,
  or somebody else.
- **Never show the landing after the leap** [E03-13]. The enemy looks for
  bodies and so should the audience.

# Part five · What it costs

By CG tier, across the twenty-two on the board:

| tier | scenes | minutes | what is in them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | 8 | 18 | the churning and Kūrma, the Deva–Asura war, the wall battle, the burning hill, the ring of kings, the pursuit, the duel |
| **4** | 6 | 10.5 | the poison, the siege arriving, the conch, seventeen, the unarmed chase, the lift |
| **3** | 7 | 11 | the pot, the chase on foot, the leap, the ash, the Yavana plain, both rides |
| **2** | 1 | 1.5 | the release of Jarasandha |

By unit, which is the more useful cut of the same data: **fire** appears in
three scenes (A1 aside), **water** in five, **animals** in nine, **crowd** in
thirteen, **chariot rig** in six, and **wire** in two. The two most expensive
single assets in the film — Kūrma and the serpent — are shared between
[E01-05] and [E01-06] and are on screen for under thirty seconds in total.

Three budgets:

- **Full.** All 22 plus A1 the flood and A5 the extended ford: ≈ 52 min of
  action in a 200-minute film, 18 minutes of it tier 5.
- **Commercial.** The 32 scenes of the 150-minute cut (73.5 min, 49%), already
  including NEW-01, 02, 07, 08, 09 and 10. Add nothing — that runtime is at its
  limit.
- **Low-budget.** Drop every tier-5 scene except the duel and the ring of
  kings; keep [E03-09] *seventeen* and [E03-06] *the battle from the wall*,
  both of which are made of reaction shots and can be delivered at tier 3. The
  film still has about 24 minutes of action and all eight devices intact, and
  the churning becomes a painted wall [E01-18] — which is where this film
  already puts it once.
