# The action

Everything in this story that moves: the twenty-two action scenes already on
the board, grouped into the twelve set pieces they actually form; the six
action scenes written for the commercial cut; the action that is implied
somewhere in the material and has never been written; and the grammar that all
of it has to obey.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| master cut (115 scenes, 197.5 min) | 22 | ≈ 41 min | 21% |
| commercial cut (75 scenes, 150.5 min) | 32 | ≈ 73.5 min | 49% |
| everything in this file | 40 | ≈ 95 min | — |

## The law that shapes every fight in this film

**The hero cannot be seen before `E06-18`.** That is not a limitation on the
action here, it is the action's whole style. Of the twenty-two action scenes on
the board, Krishna is *Not present* in six, *Shadow or sound* in five, an
*Effect* in five, a *hand* or a *voice* in three, and visible in exactly two —
the lift and the chariot going through the kings, both after the reveal.

Which means the action in this film obeys four rules the audience learns within
the first twenty minutes:

1. **The result is the shot, not the blow.** The women on the wall faint, and
   wake to an empty plain [E03-06]. Nobody watches him clear it.
2. **Somebody else does the killing.** The plough sweeps the infantry
   [E03-06]; a sleeper's glance burns the man who woke him to ash [E05-05];
   Balarama holds the ford [NEW-09]. The biggest kills in the film are not
   his.
3. **He is in the frame as a consequence** — a conch, a hand at the edge of
   frame, a silhouette against fire, a voice beside a frightened woman.
4. **He never wins by strength.** He outruns, outwaits, outthinks, or walks
   out unarmed and lets himself be chased.

## 1. The twelve set pieces on the board

### SP1 · The churning
**[E01-05] · [E01-06] · [E01-07] · tier 5, 5, 4 · ≈ 5.5 min**

A mountain is set in the sea as a churning rod and sinks; two armies stare at
the water; it rises again on something vast and curved. Then the long pull —
Devas at the tail, Asuras at the head, the mountain spinning, the serpent
coughing smoke, hands blistering — and nobody seeing what holds the mountain
up. Then the sea gives up poison instead of nectar: a black stain crossing
milk-white water faster than a man can run, fish turning white, both armies
dropping the rope and running, and one fisher child staying at the waterline
with a hand held out.

*Endurance as action.* There is no opponent in this set piece. The thing being
fought is the labour itself, and the only cut that shows him is one curved
back under a mountain.

### SP2 · The pot
**[E01-12] · tier 3 · ≈ 1.5 min**

Dhanvantari surfaces holding the amṛta and the Asuras take it before he can
speak. Hands close on the handles, the pot goes from one to the next, somebody
claims he saw it first, somebody answers with a blow — and the circle that held
through the entire churning breaks in under a minute while the gods watch from
the edge.

*A heist, not a battle.* The film's thesis in ninety seconds: seizing is not
holding.

### SP3 · The Deva–Asura war
**[E01-15] · tier 5 · ≈ 2 min**

The alliance ends. The Asuras open with illusions — fire that is not fire,
armies that vanish when struck — Bali leading from the front, Indra answering
with the thunderbolt. The Devas lose ground, then nectar-strength takes hold
and every blow lands for real. The line turns and colour comes back across the
field banner by banner.

*The only conventional battle in the film*, and it is fought by other people
entirely.

### SP4 · The siege arrives
**[E03-02] · tier 4 · ≈ 1.5 min**

Twenty-three divisions arrive in stages: a line of dust, then tents, then
banners, then a country of fires. The water-carrier puts down his bucket,
counts, gives up, and chalks the first mark on the wall because there is
nothing else to do.

*Scale measured in one man's inability to count it.*

### SP5 · The conch, the battle, and the release
**[E03-04] · [E03-06] · [E03-07] · tier 4, 5, 2 · ≈ 6.5 min**

Two chariots come down out of the sky in a column of light. Two men step up
into them, the gate opens, a conch sounds — and Jarasandha's front line steps
backwards before anything else has happened. Then the enemy closes over the
chariots, the women on the wall faint one by one, arrows take apart elephants
and horses, the plough sweeps the foot soldiers, and the women wake to a
thinning dust and an empty plain. Balarama has the king by the throat with a
rope in his other hand when a hand rises at the edge of frame and a word
follows, and his fist opens.

*The film's model battle:* sound before sight, witnesses instead of coverage,
and a moral turn at the end that costs the victory its point.

### SP6 · Seventeen
**[E03-09] · tier 4 · ≈ 2 min**

A night assault with torches, gone by morning — a mark. A fire assault with
burning carts — a mark. An elephant charge — a mark. Siege towers — a mark. A
flood — a mark. Each attempt new, each ending identical, each king released.
The wall grows seventeen strokes long, and on the last one there is no army
behind it.

*Seventeen years of war in one wall.* The single most efficient action scene in
the film and the cheapest.

### SP7 · The chase on foot and the burning hill
**[E03-11] · [E03-12] · [E03-13] · tier 3, 5, 3 · ≈ 5 min**

Jarasandha comes back with his divisions while the Yadavas are moving wealth to
the sea, and two men leave the road and run. The king rides behind them,
laughing, with a host filling the road; the two men get smaller and the host
does not. They climb Pravarṣaṇa because there is nowhere else to go, and the
king, unable to find them, sets the hillside alight. Fire runs up through the
trees. They climb above the smoke and run out of mountain. Then they jump, and
the shot follows one silhouette against the flames and stops before the ground.
Below, the king waits for bodies, is told there is no sign of them in the ash,
decides he has won and goes home.

*The only sequence in which either brother is afraid*, and the only one shot as
survival rather than war.

### SP8 · The unarmed chase and the ash
**[E05-03] · [E05-05] · [E05-12] · tier 4, 3, 3 · ≈ 4.5 min**

Kālayavana runs and does not catch him. The man ahead is always one step out of
reach, across a field, a river and a ridge, while the army falls away behind
and the country gets wilder and the shouting gets uglier — *coward, runaway,
one who will not fight* — and the man does not turn. The hills close in. There
is a dark opening in the rock. Inside, a foot lands on a sleeping man; eyes
open; the light out of them fills the cave; and when it clears there is a shape
of ash on the floor. Afterwards the gate of Mathurā opens on a plain that is
full in one shot and nearly empty in the next.

*The best action idea in the film:* the hunted is the one doing the hunting,
and the kill is somebody else's reflex.

### SP9 · Two rides
**[E06-05] · [E06-07] · tier 3, 3 · ≈ 4 min**

A Brahmin walks out before dawn with a letter in his clothes: a guard post and
a lie about a pilgrimage, a river in flood and a ferryman who wants double, a
rider coming up the road behind him who he lets pass by walking faster and then
slower. At the next village he checks that the letter is dry. Then the other
ride — a chariot leaving in the dark, the road going under the wheels, the
stars turning over, horses that do not slow, and a grey line in the east over
the plain of Vidarbha.

*Action with no opponent in it.* Two men carrying a piece of paper against a
clock, and the second ride is the interval.

### SP10 · The breakout
**[E06-19] · [E06-20] · tier 4, 5 · ≈ 2.5 min**

He does not wait for her to climb. He takes her up in front of every king and
sets her beside him; her foot is already on the board; Dāruka's hands are
already on the reins; and for half a second nobody understands what has
happened. Then the chariot goes through the ring of kings like a lion through
jackals, Balarama's men opening the way, the conch going long and clear, the
kings drawing swords too late, and a hundred horses turning at once behind her.

*The two scenes where he is fully visible* — and she is still the one the
camera is on.

### SP11 · The pursuit
**[E06-22] · tier 5 · ≈ 2.5 min**

The kings arm and chase. The Yadava commanders turn and face them; arrows fall
like rain; she sees them land and thinks for the first time about the men dying
for her. A voice beside her says, quietly, that her side will finish them
shortly. She looks back: horses going down, elephants turning, the chase
breaking into pieces.

*The one battle in the film with a romantic lead inside it*, and the only line
he speaks during action.

### SP12 · The duel
**[E06-25] · tier 5 · ≈ 3 min**

Rukmī catches up and attacks, and his bow breaks. He takes another and it
breaks. A third. Weapon after weapon, shield after shield, until he jumps down
with a sword and the sword is cut to pieces — and then the other sword turns.
She watches from the chariot while the two men she loves move toward the end of
it.

*The climax, and it is a disarming rather than a fight.* Nobody dies in the
film's biggest duel; what gets taken is a man's hair.

## 3. Action that exists in the material and has never been written

Each of these is one line, one image or one implication somewhere in the
existing scenes. Any of them can be opened into a full set piece without
inventing anything.

### A1 · The flood
**≈ 3 min · tier 5 · from one line in [E03-09]**
Jarasandha's cleverest attempt, currently a single chalk mark. A river turned
against a city: the enemy breaking a bank upstream, water coming through the
lower town at night, the water-carrier's own trade turned into the weapon. It
ends the way all seventeen end, but it is the only one where the city nearly
goes. *Shot entirely from inside the walls.* The best single addition available
to the action in this film.

### A2 · The night assault, the fire carts, the elephants, the towers
**≈ 2–3 min each · tier 4 · from [E03-09]**
The other four marks on the wall. Each is a complete set piece in one line of
the montage; opening any one of them turns the seventeen from a clever
compression into a war. *Open at most one.* Two and the montage loses the point
it is making.

### A3 · Two gates, two armies
**≈ 4 min · tier 4 · expanding [E04-01] and [E04-02]**
Thirty million men arrive at one gate while twenty-three divisions are still
camped at the other. At present this is a one-minute image and a council scene.
As action it is the film's only genuine *trap* — a city that can be crushed
between two enemies, shot as a walk along the walls from one horizon to the
other.

### A4 · The rider on the Brahmin's road
**≈ 2 min · tier 2 · expanding [E06-05]**
Rukmī sends a man after the letter. In the scene as written he rides past. As a
set piece it is the cheapest action in the film — one road, two men, no army —
and it puts the only thing that matters in the whole picture inside a chase.

### A5 · The ford, extended
**≈ 8 min · tier 4 · expanding [NEW-09]**
Balarama at the crossing is four minutes. It could carry eight, and it is the
right place to spend them: one man holding a river against a coalition while
the hero is off screen entirely, so the audience spends the longest action
sequence in the film watching somebody else pay for the hero's decision.

### A6 · The arena at Mathurā
**≈ 6 min · tier 4 · only if `C1 Kaṁsa` is built** (`CANDIDATES.md`)
A bow broken in a public street, an elephant turned loose in a gate, two
wrestlers in a ring, and a king coming off his own dais onto the floor —
all of it shot from the crowd, which is where the film shoots battles anyway.

### A7 · Pauṇḍraka's duel, and the head that goes home
**≈ 4 min · tier 4 · only if `C3 Pauṇḍraka` is built**
A man who has had the marks made, fighting with borrowed insignia, losing to
his own weapons — and his head arriving back in the city that had been calling
him Vāsudeva. The one fight in the film that can be shot conventionally,
because the visible man in it is the fake.

### A8 · The muster at Magadha
**≈ 3 min · tier 3 · only if `C6 Magadha` is built**
Not a battle: an army being assembled for the seventeenth time, with a
quartermaster who has done it sixteen times and men who are younger each time.
Action as dread.

## 4. How to shoot a fight whose hero cannot be seen

Eight devices, each already used somewhere in the film, each reusable.

| device | where it is used | what it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sound before sight** | the conch in [E03-04], [E06-20] | The enemy reacts to him before the audience sees anything. The cheapest power in the film. |
| **The witness who faints** | the women on the wall [E03-06] | Cuts out the middle of the battle and makes the gap frightening instead of cheap. |
| **Cut to the result** | the empty plain [E03-06], the plain in [E05-12] | Two shots — full, then empty — do what four minutes of coverage would do worse. |
| **Somebody else's hands** | the plough [E03-06], the glance [E05-05], the ford [NEW-09] | The biggest kills belong to other people, which is the film's argument. |
| **The hand at the edge of frame** | [E03-07] | One hand and one word reverse a victory. The whole method in a single shot. |
| **The silhouette against fire** | [E03-12], [E03-13] | The only way he is ever shown in danger, and the shot stops before the ground. |
| **The voice beside her** | [E06-22] | Turns a battle into a two-hander by putting the reassurance inside the noise. |
| **The hunted leading** | [E05-03] | A chase in which the man being chased chooses the route. Patience as action. |

And four things the action must never do:

- **No clear view of him landing a blow before [E06-18].** Not in shadow, not
  in a wide, not in one frame.
- **No slow motion on him, ever, before the reveal** — and when the reveal
  comes [E06-18], it is her eyes finding him, not a hero shot.
- **No fight he wins by being stronger.** Every win in this film is patience,
  speed, foresight, or somebody else.
- **Never show the landing after the leap** [E03-13]. The enemy looks for
  bodies and so should the audience.

## 5. What the action costs

By CG tier, across the twenty-two scenes on the board:

| tier | action scenes | minutes | what is in them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | 8 | 18 | the churning, Kūrma, the Deva–Asura war, the wall battle, the burning hill, the ring of kings, the pursuit, the duel |
| **4** | 6 | 10.5 | the poison, the siege arriving, the conch, seventeen, the unarmed chase, the lift |
| **3** | 7 | 11 | the pot, the chase on foot, the leap, the ash, the Yavana plain, both rides |
| **2** | 1 | 1.5 | the release of Jarasandha |

Three ways to budget it:

- **Full.** All 22 on the board plus A1 the flood and A5 the extended ford:
  ≈ 52 min of action in a 200-minute film, 18 minutes of it tier 5.
- **Commercial.** The 32 scenes of the 150-minute cut (73.5 min, 49%), which
  already includes NEW-01, 02, 07, 08, 09 and 10. Add nothing; it is at the
  limit of what that runtime can carry.
- **Low-budget.** Drop every tier-5 scene except the duel and the ring of
  kings, keep [E03-09] *seventeen* and [E03-06] *the battle from the wall* —
  which are tier 4 and 5 but are made of reaction shots and could be shot as
  tier 3 — and the film still has 24 minutes of action and every one of its
  eight devices intact. The churning becomes a painted wall [E01-18], which is
  where the film already puts it once.
