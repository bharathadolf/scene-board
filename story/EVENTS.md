# The eight events, told in full

`REFERENCE.md` names things. This file tells the stories. Each of the eight
events below is given as a continuous narrative — what happens, in order, from
the first scene to the last — followed by how the film chooses to tell it and
what it leaves behind for later events.

Codes in brackets are the scenes in `data/scenes.json`, so any paragraph here
can be traced to a card on the board. `SB` is the Bhāgavata Purāṇa;
*written for this film* marks a scene with no source behind it.

## E01 — Kṣīra Sāgara Manthanam

**19 scenes · ≈ 34 min · SB 8.5–8.12** *(the garland is tradition, from the
Viṣṇu Purāṇa; so is the identification of Lakṣmī with Rukmini)*

### The story

It begins with a small piece of carelessness. The sage Durvāsā offers Indra a
garland. Indra, preoccupied with his own court, drapes it over his elephant
Airāvata, who tosses it down and tramples it, and Durvāsā curses the gods to
lose everything they have [E01-01]. The curse works slowly and completely.
Prosperity drains out of heaven — colour goes out of the armour and the court,
strength goes out of the Devas — and the Asuras ride through them. Indra's
thunderbolt comes down on an enemy and does nothing. The gods run, and arrive
somewhere as suppliants for the first time [E01-02].

Brahmā takes them to Viṣṇu, and what answers them is a voice. Make an alliance
with your enemies, it says; carry a mountain to the Ocean of Milk and churn it
for the nectar of immortality; do the work, and what you cannot hold will be
held [E01-03]. So the proud go and ask. Indra bows to Bali and proposes a
joint labour, and both sides agree while hiding what they intend: the Devas
mean to keep the nectar, the Asuras expect half of it [E01-04].

Mount Mandara is brought to the shore and set in the water as a churning rod,
and it sinks. The two armies stand at the edge of the sea looking at the place
where it went down. Then it comes back up, and under it, for a moment, the
audience sees a vast curved back holding it: Kūrma, the tortoise [E01-05]. The
serpent Vāsuki is taken as the rope, Devas at the tail, Asuras at the head, and
they pull in turn for a very long time. The mountain spins, the sea convulses,
the serpent breathes smoke and fire, hands blister, strength fails — and
something nobody can see keeps both sides going [E01-06].

The first thing the ocean gives up is not a gift. The poison Hālāhala comes to
the surface and spreads over the water like black smoke; fish die, everything
living runs from the shore, and a fisher child standing at the waterline backs
away from it [E01-07]. Śiva walks down to it, cups it in his hand and drinks it.
Pārvatī's hand comes up and closes on his throat to hold it there, and his
throat turns blue. Nobody on either side says anything. Then the churning
starts again [E01-08].

With the poison held, the treasures come: Surabhi the cow, the horse
Uccaiḥśravā, the elephant Airāvata, the Kaustubha jewel, the Pārijāta tree, the
Apsarās. Each one is claimed by somebody, and what each claimant grabs says who
he is. The jewel is taken by a hand, and no one sees whose, and no one objects
[E01-09]. And then Lakṣmī rises out of the water holding a garland. Gods, sages
and Asuras arrange themselves, every one of them certain. She looks at each in
turn and passes him. At the end of the line she walks to the one who never
stepped forward and puts the garland on him, and he holds her against his chest
for good [E01-10]. The Asuras are left standing where she passed them. Vāruṇī,
the goddess of wine, rises from the sea and is given to them as a consolation,
and they take her and swallow the rest [E01-11].

Then Dhanvantari comes up out of the ocean carrying the pot of nectar, and the
Asuras take it in a rush — and immediately begin fighting each other over who
drinks first [E01-12]. While they are fighting, a beautiful woman appears.
Captivated, they hand her the ladle and ask her to serve. Mohinī separates the
two sides and serves the Devas, and the Asuras understand too late [E01-13].
One of them, Rāhu, has disguised himself as a Deva and sits in the line to
drink; the sun and the moon recognise him and point; the disc takes his head
off, and the head lives, because the nectar had already touched it [E01-14].
The alliance ends there. The Asuras attack, Bali leads them with illusions,
Indra leads with the thunderbolt, and the Devas take heaven back [E01-15].
Later, in a forest, Śiva asks to see Mohinī for himself, is bewildered by her,
follows her, and then recognises the power standing inside the form and is let
go [E01-16].

Two scenes sit outside the myth and carry it into the film. On the shore, a
fisher family lives by the water through all of it; a child dips a hand in and
brings it up clean, and later a net comes up full of silver, and the family
never learns why [E01-17]. And in Vidarbha, years later, a girl stands in front
of a painted wall at the temple of Ambikā — the mountain, the serpent, the
poison, the hand at Śiva's throat, the goddess choosing — and stops at the last
panel [E01-18]. On the night before her wedding a bard recites the whole
churning to a hall full of kings who do not notice that they are the Asuras in
it, and from the edge of the women's gallery she hears the part about the
goddess who passed every claimant [E01-19].

### How the film tells it

Krishna is not in the room for any of it, and that is the point: this event
teaches the audience how to watch the rest of the film. He is a voice from the
sky, a pair of hands taking a jewel, a back under a mountain, and a man who
does not step forward. Everything the film will later do with him is rehearsed
here at cosmic scale and without his face.

### What it leaves behind

- **The choosing.** Lakṣmī passing every claimant is Rukmini's letter, four
  events early. The mural panel she stops at [E01-18] is the exact image.
- **Seizing is not holding.** The Asuras hold the pot for about a minute before
  they turn on each other — the pattern Rukmī, Śiśupāla and Jarasandha repeat.
- **Somebody drinks the poison.** Śiva at the shore is the first of the film's
  absorptions; Krishna walking out of Dvārakā's gate alone [E04-07] is the
  second, and Rukmini at her brother's duel [E06-26] is the third.
- **The temple**, planted here, is where she will pray and where she will first
  see him.
- **The Pārijāta tree**, claimed here, arrives in Dvārakā in [E04-05].

## E02 — Bhūbhāram, the Earth's burden

**9 scenes · ≈ 14 min · SB 10.1.17–28**

### The story

Armies of arrogant kings cross a kingdom because crossing it is easier than
going around. Crops go under, wells are fouled, animals are taken, and a cow
stumbling under the shadow of a passing army goes down on her knees in a field
[E02-01]. The Earth, in the shape of that weeping cow, goes to Brahmā and tells
him what she is carrying: too many proud kings, too much weight [E02-02].

Brahmā takes her, with Śiva and the Devas, to the shore of the same Ocean of
Milk that was churned in E01, and they worship with the Puruṣa-sūkta. The water
is flat and still. Brahmā goes into trance [E02-03]. In the trance a voice from
the sky answers, and he opens his eyes and repeats what it said: the Lord
already knows what the Earth is carrying; the Devas are to take birth among the
Yadus; he himself will come as Vasudeva's son and lighten her load by his own
power over time [E02-04]. The assignments are handed out — Ananta first, as the
elder brother; Yogamāyā to carry out the work; the Devas' wives to be born
alongside them — and each one bows and accepts [E02-05]. Brahmā goes home.
Śūrasena rules from Mathurā, which becomes the Yadu capital, and a drum sounds
somewhere under the quiet of the city [E02-06].

Then, at ground level: a farmer ploughs a strip beside a road armies use. In
one image the ground is trampled flat; in the last his cow lies down in the
furrow and the plough turns clean soil [E02-07]. In a street in Kuṇḍina an army
is passing and a cow stops in the road and will not move; soldiers curse her;
an old woman standing there tells the story of the Earth who wept; and Rukmini,
on her way home, stops walking and listens [E02-08]. Later, visitors in her
father's palace sing Krishna's praises and begin not with his beauty but with
the reason for him — the Earth wept, a voice answered, a child was promised —
and she listens from the edge of the hall [E02-09].

### How the film tells it

This is the film's shortest event and its thesis: the answer arrives before the
man. Nobody petitions a hero here; a voice replies to a grievance and the reply
is a birth. It also solves the film's biggest exposition problem — the first
time the audience hears Krishna praised [E02-09], the praise has a cause
attached, so he enters the story as a consequence rather than as a celebrity.

### What it leaves behind

- **The cow**, which comes back twice: lying down in the furrow [E02-07], and
  lying down in a Dvārakā street at the very end [E07-03].
- **The reason Rukmini is interested.** Her first information about him is not
  that he is beautiful. It is that the Earth asked for him.
- **Balarama and Yogamāyā**, named in [E02-05] before either appears.

## E03 — Jarasandha's attacks on Mathurā

**15 scenes · ≈ 28 min · SB 10.50, 10.52.6–14**

### The story

Asti and Prāpti, Kaṁsa's widows, come home to their father Jarasandha and tell
him how their husband died. The grief turns into rage inside one scene, and he
stands up and calls for the army [E03-01]. Twenty-three divisions close around
Mathurā, and from the top of the wall the city's water-carrier watches tents,
banners and cooking smoke reach every horizon. On that day he makes the first
chalk mark on the wall [E03-02].

Inside, Krishna makes the decision the whole event hangs on. He will destroy
the army and leave Jarasandha alive, so that the man goes away and gathers
another one, and brings more of the Earth's burden into his hands each time he
comes. He says as much to Balarama, who sits down, unconvinced and silent
[E03-03]. Then two radiant chariots come down out of the sky with their drivers
and their weapons, the brothers ride out with a small contingent, the conch
sounds, and the enemy's front line steps backwards before it has seen anything
[E03-04]. Jarasandha calls Krishna a boy and challenges Balarama instead; a
voice answers him that courage shows in action and not in talk, and it shuts
him up [E03-05].

The battle itself is watched from the wall by the women of Mathurā. The enemy
closes around the two chariots; the chariots disappear behind the mass of them;
some of the women faint. Arrows take apart elephants, horses and chariots, the
plough sweeps the infantry, and the women come round to find the field empty of
the enemy [E03-06]. Balarama has Jarasandha on the ground and is binding him
when a word from his brother stops him, and his hands open [E03-07]. Ashamed,
Jarasandha decides on penance — and the other kings talk him out of it: it was
fate, not failure. He turns back toward Magadha [E03-08].

Then the whole war in one image. The water-carrier chalks a mark on the wall
after each siege. Each time the enemy tries something new — a night assault, a
fire assault, an elephant charge, a flood — and each time it ends the same way.
The marks reach seventeen, and the seventeenth has no army behind it [E03-09].
Between them, Krishna comes back through flowers and celebration and hands the
wealth of the battlefield to Ugrasena, the king, keeping none [E03-10].

Three scenes come later in time, after the cave in E05. Jarasandha returns with
his twenty-three divisions while the Yadavas are carrying wealth toward
Dvārakā, and the two brothers run from him on foot while he pursues them
laughing [E03-11]. They climb Mount Pravarṣaṇa; he cannot find them, so he sets
the hill on fire; two brothers stand on a burning mountain with no army, and it
is the only time in the film either of them is afraid [E03-12]. They leap off
the burning peak and vanish, and he looks for bodies, does not find any, decides
he has won, and goes home to Magadha satisfied [E03-13].

In Vidarbha the same war arrives as two conversations. Rukmī talks about
Jarasandha in front of his father and a visitor — the king who cannot be
beaten, the army that rolls on — and Rukmini, listening from the next room,
works out that her father will give way and that the Śiśupāla match is politics
[E03-14]. And in a market a soldier home from the north tells a merchant that
they beat Jarasandha seventeen times and let him go seventeen times. She stops
buying flowers and asks why. He says he does not know, and she smiles
[E03-15].

### How the film tells it

The battles are shot as effect. The camera stays on the wall with the women and
the water-carrier, so what the audience gets is the sound of the conch, the
chariots going into the mass, and the cleared field afterwards — never the man
doing it. The seventeen chalk marks compress seventeen years into one image and
turn a repetitive war into a single character study: an enemy who cannot learn,
set against an opponent who is not trying to win.

### What it leaves behind

- **"Seventeen times."** The tally is the film's first clock and Rukmini's
  first real question about him.
- **Mercy as strategy** [E03-03] — stated once here, paid off in the shaving of
  her brother [E06-27], where mercy also humiliates.
- **The burning hill** [E03-12], the lowest point of the two brothers and, in
  the commercial cut, the opening scene of the film.
- **The match with Śiśupāla** gets its political reason [E03-14], so the sale
  in E06 is not merely cruelty.

## E04 — Dvārakā rises from the sea

**10 scenes · ≈ 16 min · SB 10.50.43–58**

### The story

While the next round with Jarasandha is coming, a different army arrives.
Kālayavana brings thirty million barbarian troops to the other side of the city,
under banners the water-carrier has never seen before [E04-01]. In the council
room the problem is laid out and has no safe answer: if Jarasandha arrives while
they are fighting Kālayavana, the people of Mathurā are crushed between two
armies. Somebody puts a map on the table, and the sea is on it [E04-02]. The
decision is to stop defending and start building — a fortress no enemy can get
into, the family settled inside it, and Kālayavana dealt with afterwards.
Krishna stands on the shore at Ānarta and points at the water [E04-03].

And a city comes up out of the ocean: twelve yojanas around, gold gateways into
the sky, crystal terraces, jewelled houses, treasuries, stables, watchtowers,
temples — Viśvakarmā's hand visible in every line of it. The first gate breaks
the surface and the Yadava sailors watch the rest follow [E04-04]. Then the
world sends gifts to a man who asked for nothing: Indra the Sudharmā hall and
the Pārijāta tree out of the Churning, Varuṇa his swift horses, Kubera his eight
treasures. The tree takes root in a courtyard [E04-05].

The people are moved in a single night by yogic power. They go to sleep in
Mathurā and wake by the sea — children, cattle, carts, lamps, the whole city
relocated while it slept — and a child laughs at the waves [E04-06]. And then,
while they are all safe inside it, Krishna walks out through the gate alone,
unarmed, wearing a lotus garland, and a guard watches the gate close behind him
[E04-07].

Afterwards, the potter whose wheel was carried from Mathurā sets it down beside
the waves and turns the first pot of the new city [E04-08]. Far inland, at a
river crossing near Kuṇḍina, a ferryman and two sailors argue about whether
there is a city standing in the sea; one of them swears he has seen it and
describes the lamps burning all night; Rukmini listens from the back of the boat
[E04-09]. That night, having never seen the sea, she traces the walls on a
painted cloth with her finger, closes her eyes, and the film shows what she
imagines — the place as she needs it to be rather than as it is [E04-10].

### How the film tells it

The only piece of pure spectacle in the picture, and it is a creation rather
than a battle: the hero ends a seventeen-year war by moving the target. It is
also the event where the film's two halves touch — the same city that rises out
of the water here is the one she walks into at the end, and her imagined
version [E04-10] exists so that the real one [E07-02] can be compared with it.

### What it leaves behind

- **The lone walk out of the gate** [E04-07] — the single image of him that
  Rukmini will carry, and the start of E05.
- **Dvārakā itself**, the place the film ends in.
- **The potter**, who will stand in a doorway in [E07-03].
- **Her picture of the sea**, made before she ever sees it.

## E05 — Mucukunda and the man on foot

**16 scenes · ≈ 27 min · SB 10.50.43–10.52.5**

### The story

Nārada, who enjoys this sort of thing, goes to Kālayavana's camp and tells him
exactly how to recognise Vāsudeva: the garland, the mark on the chest, the dark
skin. Then he leaves, smiling, and Kālayavana repeats the marks aloud to himself
[E05-01]. Outside Mathurā a man walks out of the city unarmed in a lotus
garland. Kālayavana checks him against the list, recognises him, gets off his
horse and drops his weapon, deciding to kill him with his hands [E05-02].

What follows is a chase in which the hunted is doing the hunting. Krishna stays
just ahead of him — always a step short of being caught, never far enough ahead
to be lost — across fields and rivers and up into the hills, while Kālayavana
shouts at him for a coward and he lets it be said [E05-03]. The hills close in
and he goes into a cave. Kālayavana follows him into the dark and finds a man
lying asleep on the floor; certain that this is Krishna pretending, he raises
his foot [E05-04]. He kicks the sleeper. The sleeper's eyes open, and he looks
at him, and Kālayavana is ash [E05-05].

The sleeper is Mucukunda, a king of the Ikṣvāku line who once fought for the
Devas against the Asuras. When they offered him a boon he asked for undisturbed
sleep and the power to burn whoever woke him, and when Kārttikeya took over as
their general he was released and chose sleep over liberation [E05-06]. Now a
figure steps into the light at the mouth of the cave, and a king who has slept
through ages looks at a man of impossible radiance and wonders whether this is
the Supreme Lord himself. The scene is his face [E05-07].

He asks who he is, and a voice answers him: my births are countless; in this age
I came as Vasudeva's son to protect dharma; I killed Kaṁsa; your glance burned
Kālayavana; and I am here because you prayed for me once, long ago [E05-08]. So
Mucukunda prays — about the delusion of worldly happiness, the rarity of a human
birth, the waste of a kingship, death that waits like a snake, the futility of
power, and freedom found in good company — and asks for nothing but to serve
[E05-09]. Boons are offered to him and he refuses every one of them, and is
told that the offer was a test and that he passed it [E05-10]. He walks out of
the cave, judges that the age he has woken into is not his, looks back once, and
goes north to Badarikāśrama [E05-11]. Krishna returns to Mathurā, destroys the
barbarian army, and sends its wealth on toward Dvārakā [E05-12].

Around the edges: a goatherd grazing his flock near the mountain sees smoke come
out of a cave and, later, a sage walking past, and when the siege lifts his
goats walk back onto the road [E05-13]. At a roadside well near Kuṇḍina a
herdsman tells a friend that a Yavana ran into a cave after a man and only smoke
came out; Rukmini, filling a jar, asks which man, and then asks whether he was
wearing a garland [E05-14]. At the temple of Ambikā a priest recites
Mucukunda's prayer to her mother and to her — the king who gave up a kingdom
after seeing him — and she asks him to say it again [E05-15]. And in the palace
a visitor trading gossip lists the marks by which Vāsudeva is known, exactly as
Nārada gave them to Kālayavana; her maid giggles; she checks each one against
what she has heard and then asks about a mark the visitor cannot explain
[E05-16].

### How the film tells it

This is the most dangerous event for the film's one rule, because it is the
event in which somebody looks straight at him — so the film shoots the looking
instead of the man. Mucukunda's face is the whole of [E05-07], and the voice in
[E05-08] is the closest thing to a self-description anyone gets. The biggest
kill in the picture is not even his: he walks, and somebody else's glance does
it.

### What it leaves behind

- **The checklist.** Nārada's marks [E05-01], repeated in the palace
  [E05-16], are what the audience is holding when they finally see his face in
  [E06-18].
- **Mucukunda's prayer**, which reaches Rukmini through a priest [E05-15] and
  gives her plea for her brother its moral ground.
- **Accepted shame.** He is called a coward for a whole sequence and lets it
  stand, which is what makes the patience of the seventeen sieges legible.
- **The garland**, by which she identifies him in a rumour [E05-14] before she
  ever identifies him by sight.

## E06 — Rukmiṇī Kalyāṇam, the letter and the abduction

**31 scenes · ≈ 60 min · SB 10.52.16–10.54.52**

### The story

A listener asks a bard how Krishna won Rukmiṇī, having heard that it was a
marriage by capture: *they say he stole her*. The bard's answer is the film's
whole argument — "she wrote the letter" — and then he tells it [E06-01].

In Bhīṣmaka's hall, visitors sing about Krishna's qualities and his valour, and
Rukmini, listening from the edge of the room, decides that this is the man she
will marry. Somewhere else, he reaches the same conclusion about her [E06-02].
But her brother Rukmī hates Krishna, blocks the family's inclination, and
arranges a match with Śiśupāla of Chedi instead; her father, who favours
Krishna, gives way out of love for his son; and she is overruled in her own
house [E06-03].

So she does the thing nobody has guarded against. She sends for a brāhmaṇa she
trusts, and she writes: I have chosen you, and no sensible woman, having heard
what I have heard, would choose another; if you do not come I will give up my
life; come in secret; and here is how — the procession to the temple of Ambikā
before the wedding. She seals it and hands it away [E06-04]. The Brahmin
carries it along a very long road, past a guard post, across a river, ahead of a
man Rukmī sends after him, and gets the letter to the other side dry, thinking
all the way that it is heavier than gold [E06-05]. At Dvārakā he is received and
honoured and fed; Krishna presses his feet, asks after his welfare, his duties
and his king, and offers help before any of this is mentioned; and then the
letter is read, and he is asked to decide quickly, and he smiles [E06-06]. He
asks when the wedding is, orders the chariot, and Dāruka brings it, and they
ride from Ānarta to Vidarbha in one night, dark road and hooves and then dawn
coming up over the plain [E06-07].

In Kuṇḍina the wedding is being built around her. The city is washed and
decorated, the rituals are performed, the gifts go out, and she is bathed and
adorned — the preparation shot as a kind of imprisonment — and she hides
something in a fold of the dress [E06-08]. Damaghoṣa arrives with an army;
Jarasandha, Śālva, Dantavakra, Vidūratha, Pauṇḍraka and thousands of kings come
in as Śiśupāla's allies and swear aloud that if Krishna comes for the bride they
will fight him [E06-09]. Balarama, afraid of what his brother is about to walk
into, marches for Kuṇḍina with a large army of his own [E06-10].

And then she waits. The hour is close and the Brahmin has not come back. She
invents reasons — that she has offended Brahmā, or Śiva — and consoles herself
that the time has not actually passed yet, and prays to the very gods she is
afraid of [E06-11]. Her left eye twitches, and her arm, and her thigh: good
signs. The Brahmin walks in smiling before he says anything, carrying the
message and the promise [E06-12]. She bows to him. At the palace gate her father
receives Krishna and Balarama with honour and seats the other kings by rank
[E06-13]. In the streets the citizens look at Krishna and wish out loud that she
may get him, offering their own merit for it, and a woman lifts a garland toward
her window [E06-14].

The procession goes out, guarded: her mothers, her friends, musicians and bards,
drums and conches and horns, and a ring of soldiers around her all the way to
the temple of Ambikā, where she steps over the threshold [E06-15]. She washes
and goes in, and brāhmaṇa women guide her through the worship, and she asks the
goddess for Krishna as her husband and receives the blessing and the prasāda —
with the Churning painted on the wall behind her [E06-16]. Then she breaks her
vow of silence, speaks, and walks out through the forecourt full of kings, led
by a maid, and the kings are stunned by the sight of her [E06-17]. She comes on
shyly, glancing at them, and her eyes find him. **This is the first time the
audience sees his face, at minute 171 of 198** [E06-18]. He takes her up in
front of all of them and lifts her onto the chariot, and her foot leaves the
ground because she has already decided it would [E06-19]. Then he drives out
through the ring of kings with Balarama in front of him, like a lion walking its
kill out of a circle of jackals, and the conch sounds [E06-20]. Behind them,
Jarasandha and the rest cannot bear it and say so — *cowherds have taken our
honour* — while Śiśupāla stands there in the groom's clothes looking at his
empty hands [E06-21].

The kings arm and come after them. The Yadava commanders turn and face them on
the plain; arrows come down; she is frightened for her husband's army, and his
voice tells her that her side will finish them shortly, and Gada and Saṅkarṣaṇa
and the others take apart the horses and elephants and chariots [E06-22].
Jarasandha looks at what is left of his side, withdraws, and consoles the
wrecked Śiśupāla on the roadside: victory and defeat belong to time, and the
kings will win when time turns. They go home [E06-23]. But Rukmī cannot let it
go. He follows with a division of his own and swears in front of the kings that
he will not enter Kuṇḍina again until he has killed Krishna and brought his
sister back [E06-24].

He attacks. His bows are broken one after another, his weapons and his shield
destroyed; he jumps down with a sword and the sword is cut to pieces; and then
the other sword comes up to kill him, while she watches the two men she loves
try to kill each other [E06-25]. She goes down at Krishna's feet and asks for
her brother's life, and the sword comes down [E06-26]. Instead of killing him,
Krishna ties him with cloth and shaves off part of his hair and his moustache —
mercy and humiliation in the same stroke — and she looks away from it [E06-27].
Balarama cuts Rukmī loose and turns on his brother: this is not worthy of our
family. Krishna does not answer him [E06-28]. Then Balarama sits with her and
tells her that people carry their own joy and sorrow, that the self is not the
body, and that a warrior's duties are sometimes ugly, and she wipes her face
[E06-29]. Rukmī, unable to go home shaved, builds the city of Bhojakaṭa on a
river plain and stays there [E06-30].

Back in Kuṇḍina the leftovers: the flower vendor's garlands are worn by a
different bride at another house, the weaver's wedding cloth is carried off on a
chariot, and the ferryman takes one Yadava soldier across the river and nobody
else [E06-31].

### How the film tells it

The event is built so that the abduction is her plan being executed. Everything
before the temple is her decision and her waiting; the lift [E06-19] takes one
minute and is the shortest beat in the sequence. The withholding of his face for
171 minutes pays off inside a glance, with no line of dialogue — and because the
audience has been given Nārada's checklist of marks [E05-01, E05-16], they
identify him at the same moment she does.

### What it leaves behind

- **Her letter**, which the film treats as the title of the story: the bard
  sings it at the end [E07-06].
- **The shaving**, which costs more than it gains — Balarama's rebuke
  [E06-28], her brother's absence from her wedding [E07-05], and Bhojakaṭa.
- **Śiśupāla's humiliation** [E06-21], which E08 turns into the countdown to
  his death.
- **Jarasandha**, alive and already consoling himself [E06-23].

## E07 — The final episode: together

**8 scenes · ≈ 9 min · SB 10.54.53–60**

### The story

The text says that he married her according to Vedic rites and took her home to
Dvārakā. The film shows none of the wedding. It cuts from the road, right after
Balarama's consolation, straight to the city, and leaves the ceremony as a gap,
with the sea appearing at the edge of the road [E07-01]. She has lived inland
all her life, so the ocean arrives from the chariot all at once, with a city
standing in it — and she measures it against the one she drew with her finger on
a painted cloth [E07-02].

Dvārakā is decorated at its doors. Guests from many clans are glad, citizens
bring jewelled gifts, lamps stand in doorways and the streets are covered in
flowers. In the doorways are the people who carried the whole film without ever
knowing what they were carrying: the flower vendor, the water-carrier, the
potter, the goatherd, the farmer, the fisher family. A cow lies down in the
middle of the street. The wall is clean, and the water-carrier raises a hand
beside it [E07-03].

In her new room she sets down the lamp she carried from Kuṇḍina and finds a
matching lamp already lit, and puts hers beside it [E07-04]. In the middle of
the celebration there is a place laid for a brother who will not be coming
[E07-05]. In a courtyard a bard sings the story of the abduction, the way the
text says it was sung all over the country — and he includes the letter, which
makes it her side of the story, and the kings hear it that way [E07-06]. On a
terrace above the sea, one of the two of them finally says something: either his
answer to her letter, or her account of what she saw at the temple door, which
is the letter in reverse [E07-07]. And then they stand side by side at the edge
of the water, in profile or from behind, with her at the visual centre, and the
shot is held a little longer than any shot of Krishna anywhere in the film
[E07-08].

### How the film tells it

Nine minutes, no wedding, and the camera still refuses to make him the subject.
Every carrier from the preceding three hours gets one doorway, so the audience
can see the result land on the people who never knew the cause — and the clean
wall is the last answer to the seventeen chalk marks. The empty place [E07-05]
keeps the ending honest: she got what she chose, and it cost her a brother.

### What it leaves behind

Nothing. This is the end of every cut of the film, and nothing is placed after
it.

## E08 — The backstories

**7 scenes · ≈ 12 min · Mahābhārata, Sabhā Parva 40–45; SB 10.74; SB 3.15–16;
SB 7.1**

### The story

These seven scenes are the pasts the main story stands on; the board can place
them anywhere, and most cuts use only some of them.

In the palace of Chedi a child is born with three eyes and four arms, and a
voice out of the air says that the extra marks will disappear when the child is
set on the lap of the person who will cause his death [E08-01]. Krishna visits,
takes the infant on his lap, and the arms and the eye vanish while his mother
watches — so the woman whose child has just been made whole understands in the
same second who has done it and what else he is going to do [E08-02]. She begs
him to spare her son, and he promises to tolerate a hundred offences [E08-03].
Behind that, further back: the four Kumāras come to Vaikuṇṭha and are stopped at
the gate by Jaya and Vijaya, who are cursed to be born in the world for it, and
are then offered a choice — seven births as devotees, or three as enemies. They
choose three [E08-04].

And after the abduction: Śiśupāla, still dressed as a groom, finds the bride
gone and works out that she left willingly, which is the part that hardens into
hatred [E08-05]. Balarama, in one scene, is the man who defends his brother with
an army and then rebukes him over the shaving: two loyalties inside one person
[E08-06]. And Rukmī's whole arc compressed into a single scene — a brother who
arranges a match, swears an oath, loses a duel and then builds a city so that he
never has to go home [E08-07].

### How the film tells it

Backstory as clockwork rather than exposition. Śiśupāla's hundred offences give
a count that nobody on screen is keeping but the audience is; Jaya and Vijaya
put a curse behind him that rhymes with Durvāsā's curse at the very start of
E01, so the film opens and closes its mythology with the same mechanism.

### What it leaves behind

- **A countdown**, running under every scene Śiśupāla appears in.
- **The curse structure**, which ties E08 back to [E01-01].
- **A one-scene version of Rukmī** [E08-07] for cuts that cannot afford his
  full arc.

## How the eight fit together

In story order: the Churning (E01) is ages before anything; the Earth's
petition (E02) is the generation before the hero; Jarasandha's sieges (E03) run
for seventeen years; Kālayavana and the cave (E05) interrupt them; Dvārakā
(E04) is built in one night in the middle of that; the burning hill [E03-11 to
E03-13] comes after the cave; the letter and the abduction (E06) take about a
week; the ending (E07) is one day; and E08 reaches back behind all of it.

The film does not use that order. It is non-linear, and the thing it keeps in
order is a different sequence altogether: **a woman hears about a man, decides,
writes, is taken away at her own request, loses a brother for it, and arrives
somewhere she had only imagined.** Everything in E01 to E05 is, structurally,
what reaches her — a mural, a recital, a soldier in a market, a herdsman at a
well, a priest, a sailor on a ferry. Two of those events (E01, E02) tell her why
he exists; two more (E03, E05) tell her what he does; one (E04) tells her where
he lives. She assembles him out of rumour for three hours, and then the film
lets her see him once, in [E06-18], at the same moment the audience does.
